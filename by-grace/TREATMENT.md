# By Grace — two watercolor films (Watercolor Brush)

Two silent 16:9 background films, about 3:00 each, painted in the Watercolor Brush style, made to sit under two gospel songs on the theme of *By Grace, Still Standing*: a night of loss, a mother's prayer, an empty cupboard, a closed door, a long wait, then grace, forgiveness and a morning. No lyrics on screen, no voice, no music in the file: the song is the soundtrack.

Both films share one section map, built on a slow gospel ballad grid (72 BPM, 4/4, bar = 3.333 s, 54 bars = 3:00). Big changes land on section boundaries, so whichever song goes underneath, the picture breathes with it.

## Film 1 — Mosaic

### Three structures considered
1. **A long handscroll** from storm to dawn, one lateral dolly. A journey, clear — but it is the first idea this style suggests, and one move for three minutes goes flat.
2. **A stack of journal pages**, one per memory, turned like a diary. Strong grammar, but each page wipes the last; nothing accumulates.
3. **A mosaic sheet** (chosen): 21 small paintings in a 7 × 3 grid, painted one per line of the story, left to right in time. The background washes of the tiles run from storm indigo (left) to dawn gold (right), so the finished sheet is also one sky turning to morning. In each hook the camera pulls back and a single large standing figure is glazed across the whole sheet in a transparent umber, a little more each hook: feet and ground, then body, then the lifted head. The pieces of the story become one person standing. That is the turn, and it is a move only transparent watercolour can make: the glaze darkens where it crosses, but every tile still shows through.

### Logline and arc
A night of small sorrows, painted one square at a time, turns out to have been the portrait of someone still standing.
- **Setup:** a single candle in the dark; the losses, the prayer, the storm, the empty cupboard.
- **Turn:** a door opens with light; the pull-back shows the figure beginning under the tiles.
- **End:** sunrise glazes the sheet; everything mists back to paper except the standing figure and the vermilion heart at its centre.

### Benchmark
Learn from naturalist plates (one subject per tile, a one-word hand note under each). Don't learn the gallery look of "a grid of thumbnails": every tile is painted in, live, at a size you can read.

### Tiles (one-word hand notes in the gutter)
0 candle · 1 seed in the soil · 2 pillow and window rain · 3 praying hands, lamp gone out · 4 storm over a house · 5 nearly empty cupboard · 6 door opening with light · 7 closed door and a chair · 8 open garden gate · 9 an empty court at dusk · 10 the seed now a tree · 11 kneeling, head lifted · 12 wings rising · 13 open hands · 14 rose on a thorny stem · 15 two figures walking away · 16 bread and cup on a table · 17 heartbeat line (vermilion) · 18 fire · 19 a room of raised hands · 20 sunrise over hills.

The accent vermilion is reserved for the flame / heart that recurs: candle, heartbeat, fire, sun, and the heart of the standing figure.

### Cue map
Every tile starts its paint-in on a bar line. Verse tiles: 1.5 bars each (5.0 s): 0.8 s camera move, 3.4 s painting, 0.8 s hold. Bridge tiles: 1 bar each. Glaze stages start on the first downbeat of each hook and paint over 4 bars.

## Film 2 — The Window

### Three structures considered
1. **A road trip** across a painted country. Too far from the theme's quiet interior.
2. **Anatomy of one object** (a house dissected into rooms). Clever, but cold.
3. **One locked view through time** (chosen): a small house, its yard and a gate, seen from across the lane. The camera barely moves; time does. The storm night, the lamp in the window, the light going out, a seed pressed into the yard, seasons passing over a growing tree, the gate opening, and a figure standing under the grown tree at sunrise. A change in time on a locked view is the native move.

### Logline and arc
One house, one night that lasts for years, and a seed that keeps growing until the morning comes.
- **Setup:** night, rain, one lit window (the mother praying).
- **Turn:** the lamp goes out; a hand plants a seed in the dark yard; the seasons start turning.
- **End:** the grown tree at dawn, the gate open, a figure standing on the path; the window lit again, warmer.

## Both films
- **Sound design table:** none in the file by request (the user's songs are the soundtrack). Silences and accelerations are designed into the picture on the section map so the songs can carry them.
- **Type:** no lyrics and no titles. Film 1 has tiny one-word hand notes (Caveat) under its tiles, the field-journal annotation layer the style asks for. Film 2 has no text at all.
- **Frame rate:** 30 fps (the slow brush reads smoothly; rendering three minutes at 60 fps would double the time without changing what is seen at this speed).
