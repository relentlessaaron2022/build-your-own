When to treat it as urgent

  • Sewage backed up into the house, or drains that stay slow after the yard has dried
  • A sewage smell around the tank or drain field once the rain has stopped
  • The ground over the tank has sunk or settled
  • A septic pump or alarm is going off
  • The drain field stays wet and spongy long after the rest of the yard is dry

If sewage has backed up into a basement or crawl space, the company's published storm guidance is to open things up for fresh air, wear gloves, clean the area, and disinfect (they note the EPA suggestion of about half a cup of chlorine bleach per gallon of water). Stay away from wet electrical parts if you have a pump or alarm, and don't dig around a wet tank or field.

After a heavy storm, they may start with questions rather than a truck — including whether the ground is still too wet to work safely, and whether the tank itself is flooded. That's not a brush-off; it's how they avoid making a saturated system worse. If you're in Alpharetta and you're not sure whether to wait or to call it an emergency, call and tell them what you see.
