What a real inspection looks at

When they inspect a system for a sale, they check the basics the EPA lists:

  • Sludge and scum levels inside the tank
  • The condition of the tank, the baffles, and the inlet and outlet pipes
  • Signs of backup, such as staining above the outlet
  • Signs of leaking, such as a water level sitting too low
  • The distribution box, so each drain line gets an even share
  • The drain field, including soggy ground, standing water, or odor

In red clay, the field is where trouble likes to hide. A strip of grass greener than the rest of the yard, or ground that stays mushy days after a rain, is worth taking seriously.

If you're buying or selling in Alpharetta

Ask the seller for pumping and service records, the age of the system and any repairs, and the permit or as-built drawing. Fulton County Environmental Health, including the North Fulton Regional Health Center office, can often help with that drawing. If a general inspector doesn't include septic, book a separate septic inspection. Sellers who pump and inspect before the sign goes up hand the buyer cleaner records and find problems on their own schedule. A freshly pumped tank looks tidy, but pumping alone doesn't prove the system works — ask for a written report.

Questions homeowners ask

Does a home inspection include the septic system?

Often it does not. Action Septic tells buyers to ask the home inspector point blank whether septic is included, and to book a separate septic inspection if it isn't.

Will I get a written report?

The company recommends asking for a written report you can keep with the household records or hand to the other side of a sale.
