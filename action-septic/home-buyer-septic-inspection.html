Fall is a busy stretch for house hunting around Alpharetta, Milton, and Johns Creek, and plenty of those homes sit on septic. Action Septic Tank Service gets called to a lot of yards in the middle of a sale — sometimes by the buyer's agent, sometimes by the seller. A little homework up front keeps a closing on track.

For buyers

Before you fall for the backyard, ask the seller for pumping and service records with dates, the age of the system and any repairs, and the permit or as-built drawing showing where the tank and drain field sit. The EPA's homebuyer guide points people to the local health department for that drawing. Here, that's Fulton County Environmental Health, and the North Fulton Regional Health Center office can steer you. Older homes in Roswell and Sandy Springs sometimes come with a thin file. Don't panic; it just means the inspection carries more weight.

Ask your home inspector point blank whether the septic system is included. If it isn't, book a separate septic inspection. Flushing toilets on a walkthrough tells you very little about the yard.

What the inspection should cover

When Action Septic inspects a system for a sale, they check sludge and scum levels; the tank, baffles, and inlet and outlet pipes; backup staining; signs of leaking; the distribution box; and the drain field. Pumping is often part of a good inspection because it lets them see the tank walls, but a freshly pumped tank by itself doesn't prove the system works. Ask for a written report.

For sellers

If you're listing in Cumming, Milton, or Alpharetta, think about having the tank pumped and inspected before the sign goes up. Locate the lid, or add a riser so it's easy to reach. Pull together every receipt you can find. Fix small issues like a cracked lid or a worn baffle before they land on someone else's report.

A problem doesn't have to kill the deal. A broken baffle or cracked line is usually a quicker fix than a failing field, which is where buyers and sellers tend to negotiate. A clear look from a licensed contractor gives both sides something solid. Call 770-922-1434.

Questions homeowners ask

Is pumping enough for a closing?

No. Action Septic is clear that pumping and inspecting are two different things. Pumping may be part of a thorough inspection, but a pumped tank alone doesn't prove the system works. Ask for a written report.

Where do I get an as-built drawing?

The company points buyers to Fulton County Environmental Health, including the North Fulton Regional Health Center office, and notes that the EPA's homebuyer guidance sends people to the local health department for that record.
