Signs your septic tank needs pumping.

Don't wait for sewage in the house. These are the warning signs Action Septic Tank Service tells North Atlanta homeowners to watch for. Call 770-922-1434.

A septic tank doesn't send a polite reminder when the sludge and scum have climbed too high. The house and the yard usually tell on it first. Action Septic Tank Service has published these kinds of warnings across its official guides:

  • Slow drains or gurgling toilets
  • Foul odors in or around the house, tank, or drain field
  • Pooling effluent around the tank or field
  • Patches of grass that are greener or spongier than the rest of the yard
  • Drains that slow after a heavy rain, then recover a few days later
  • A pump alarm that tends to go off during storms (if you have one)
  • A tank that fills back up to the outlet unusually fast after it was just pumped
  • Sewage backing up into the house

Some of those signs can also mean a broken line, a failed baffle, or a field that's already in trouble — not only a tank that's due. That's why a pumping visit should include a look at accessible parts, and why symptoms that continue after pumping deserve another call.

If you can't remember the last pumping, treat that as a sign too. Call 770-922-1434. For urgent backups, see emergency service.

Questions homeowners ask

What if I have no records?

Don't delay service just because you can't find receipts or a drawing. Many homeowners, especially after buying a house, don't know the tank location or history. The technician can still do the work.

Is greener grass always a septic problem?

A strip of extra-green or spongy grass over the drain field is one of the signs the company tells people to take seriously, especially if it lingers after rain. It's a reason to call, not a diagnosis by itself.
