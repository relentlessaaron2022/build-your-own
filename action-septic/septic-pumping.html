Septic tank pumping, explained before we hook up the hose.

Pumping removes the sludge and scum a tank is designed to hold — before those solids reach the drain field. Call Action Septic Tank Service at 770-922-1434.

Wastewater from sinks, toilets, showers, and the laundry line flows into the septic tank. Heavier solids settle as sludge. Oils and grease rise as scum. The clearer liquid in the middle goes out to the drain field. Bacteria break down some of the solids, but not all of them. What's left has to be pumped out by a professional.

Action Septic Tank Service describes a pumping appointment in practical steps: gather any records you have (don't delay if you can't find them), locate and access the tank, measure and remove the accumulated material, look at accessible baffles, filters, pipes, and tank surfaces, then close the tank and talk through the findings. Only trained professionals should clean or repair septic tanks. Tanks can contain dangerous gases. Homeowners should never enter one.

What to ask before the crew leaves

  • How full was the tank?
  • Were the sludge and scum levels unusually high?
  • Were the baffles and accessible components intact?
  • Were there signs of leakage or backup?
  • Was the drain field showing visible trouble?
  • When should the system be checked or pumped again?

Keep the invoice with your household records. Pumping is maintenance, not a cure for every problem. If slow drainage, odors, standing water, or backups continue afterward, the system may need further evaluation — see inspections and repair in Alpharetta.
