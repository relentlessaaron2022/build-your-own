Solids do not disappear in a septic tank. Sludge settles, scum floats, and what's left in the middle moves out to the drain field. If the tank isn't pumped, those solids rise until they head for the field — and in North Fulton's red clay, a clogged field is a much harder problem than a routine pumping.

How often should an Alpharetta tank be pumped?

On its official site, Action Septic has recommended annual inspections and pumping residential tanks every two to three years, with commercial tanks usually needing more frequent service. In later guides, the company also cites the U.S. EPA's general guidance: inspect a household system at least every three years and pump every three to five years, depending on tank size, how many people live there, water use, and garbage-disposal use.

Questions homeowners ask

How often do you recommend pumping?

Action Septic has recommended pumping residential tanks every two to three years and inspecting annually, and also points homeowners to EPA guidance of every three to five years depending on the house and tank. Commercial tanks usually need more frequent pumping.

Is a pumping the same as an inspection?

No. Pumping removes accumulated solids. An inspection evaluates the tank, baffles, pipes, and often the drain field. Pumping can be part of a good inspection because it lets the technician see the tank walls, but a freshly pumped tank alone does not prove the system works.
