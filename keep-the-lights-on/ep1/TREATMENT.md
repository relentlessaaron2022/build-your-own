# Keep the Lights On — Ep. 1 "$1,625" — treatment

Style: **1930s Rubber Hose Cartoon** (`rubber-hose`). Vertical 1080×1920, 24 fps, 30.0 s. A 3:4 film gate (1080×1440, rounded corners) centred in the frame, black above and below, the way the style's 4:3 gate sits inside 16:9, turned upright.
No narration and no subtitles. The money arrives as period title cards inside the gate. Original score: a hot-jazz trio played sly and slow.

**As built** (`story.js` is the source of truth; these timings replace the planning numbers below): pings at 1.33 / 4.00 / 6.67 s (beats 2, 6, 10); cards 1.33–3.67, 4.00–6.33, 6.67–9.83; shot 1 runs to 10.00; lock at 10.00, tuck 10.67–11.33, pillow "pomf" 11.33; tiptoe steps 13.33–18.00; band stops 20.00–23.33; peek 27.33; blink and iris 28.67–29.00. The lock is a finger on the side button. The phone ends tucked down behind the front pillow and peeks over its top.

## Three structures

1. **Three pings, a lock, a blink (chosen).** The phone is the hero: it wakes three times as money lands, gets hidden under a breathing pillow, and has the last word with one blink before the iris shuts. The humans stay seen from behind or in silhouette. A rhythm of three, a turn and a button: the gag shape the medium is built for.
2. **She's the hero, a tiptoe routine.** Every move of hers Mickey-moused. Charming, but the camera would have to live on her face, which the story forbids.
3. **The apartment is the hero.** The whole room breathes; the pillow, the blinds and the steam react to every ping. Lovely texture, but no single character to follow at thumbnail size.

Structure 1 keeps the faces hidden by design, so the reactions all go to an object, which is exactly what this style does best.

## Logline and arc

While he showers, her phone keeps waking up with money she can't explain, and she puts it to bed before walking into the steam like nothing happened.

- **Setup** (0–10 s): the phone on the bed, pinging awake three times; three title cards.
- **Turn** (10–20 s): she locks it, hides it under the pillow and tiptoes toward the steam.
- **Button** (20–30 s): the band stops for the embrace behind the curtain; steam swallows the frame; back on the pillow, the phone peeks out, the light blinks once, iris out on the blink.

## Benchmark

- **Early-1930s sound cartoons with a living bedroom** (furniture that breathes, an object that reacts to a secret). Learn: medium shots, gags carried by one object, the band answering every action, the iris as punctuation. Don't take: any character, design, gag or melody.
- **Pre-Code film staging of intimacy** (silhouettes on a curtain, a door closing, a cut away). Learn: suggestion over display. Don't take: any shot.

## Title cards

Period cards: a small near-black plate, white double-rule border, dot-and-ring corners, IM Fell English SC in paper white. Placed low in the gate (the action is high), popping on and off, weaving with the film.
- `+ $1,200.00` 2.0–4.0 · `+ $400.00` 5.0–7.0 · `TIP: "don't stop talking"` 7.5–9.33 (held longest for reading).

## Cast and design

- **The phone:** a rounded slab standing on the bed, black body, white screen face; pie eyes and a grin on the screen; noodle arms with white gloves; a small notification light on top. Asleep: closed lid lines. Original design; no real phone brand.
- **Her:** seen only from behind or as hands: an oversized nightshirt, a big puffy night bonnet, noodle arms, white gloves, round shoes. Never a face. Not a flapper silhouette.
- **Him:** only a silhouette behind the shower curtain, and his long noodle arms.
- **The room:** grey watercolour wallpaper with a small diamond pattern, a brass bed frame, two plump pillows, slatted blinds with streetlight stripes and tall cartoon buildings outside, a bathroom door with steam.
