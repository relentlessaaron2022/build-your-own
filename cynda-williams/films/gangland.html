A life in progress

This timeline is in progress. Every entry is tied to a public source. Dates, venues, and credits we could not verify are marked as gaps — they are not filled in. Private family details and unsourced relationship gossip are left off.

  1. Chicago and Muncie — early life

    Born Cindy Ann Williams in Chicago, Illinois. Wikipedia, citing a 1984 Northside High School yearbook and a 1990 Entertainment Weekly profile, says she was raised in the Pullman neighborhood on the city’s South Side, spent parts of her early childhood in Indiana singing in her grandfather’s church, attended Bennett Elementary, later moved with her mother to Muncie, Indiana, and graduated from Northside High School in 1984. The same EW profile names her parents as Charles, a Chicago police officer, and Beverly, a medical lab technician.

    Film databases including IMDb and TMDB list her birth date as May 17, 1966. Wikipedia’s infobox lists Chicago but does not publish a birth date. EW, in August 1990, described her as 24, which is consistent with 1966, as is a 1984 high-school graduation. This timeline records 1966 as the date those databases use, and leaves the record open if a different official date is supplied.

    Wikipedia Entertainment Weekly, Aug. 24, 1990 IMDb TMDB

  2. 1987–1989 — Ball State University

    Williams studied theater at Ball State University in Indiana, was crowned Ms. Ball State in 1987, and graduated in 1989. The 1990 EW profile says that as a theater major she played leads in Evita, West Side Story, and A Raisin in the Sun. Contemporary Theatre, Film and Television, reprinted at Encyclopedia.com, also lists stage appearances in A Chorus Line and Two by Two, without dates or venues.

    Entertainment Weekly, Aug. 24, 1990 Wikipedia Encyclopedia.com

  3. 1989 — New York, and a new first name

    After graduation she moved to New York, worked as a restaurant hostess and later a bookkeeper, and found an agent, according to EW. Wikipedia says that in 1989 she changed her first name to Cynda to avoid a Screen Actors Guild conflict with the actress Cindy Williams of Laverne & Shirley.

    Entertainment Weekly, Aug. 24, 1990 Wikipedia Encyclopedia.com

  4. 1990 — Film debut in Spike Lee’s Mo’ Better Blues

    Williams made her feature debut as the singer Clarke Bentancourt (spellings of the surname vary across sources). She also sang “Harlem Blues” on the soundtrack. For the week ending November 17, 1990, Billboard’s Hot R&B Singles chart, as compiled by ChartVault, lists the Columbia single at No. 9, with a peak of No. 9. (Some later biographies, including an Amazon author bio and a 2020 magazine profile, have called the record a No. 1. The chart table does not support that. This site uses No. 9.)

    Wikipedia says the single’s success led to a Sony album that was later shelved after internal disagreements. A 1990 EW profile said Spike Lee had signed her for Jungle Fever; she does not appear in that film’s credited cast. That planned role is recorded here as a gap, not as a credit.

    Wikipedia Entertainment Weekly, Aug. 24, 1990 ChartVault / Billboard, Nov. 17, 1990 Universal Pictures At Home

  5. 1991–1993 — One False Move and an Independent Spirit nomination

    She starred as Lila “Fantasia” Walker in Carl Franklin’s One False Move. Sources split the year: IMDb and AllMovie often list 1991; Wikipedia and Encyclopedia.com list 1992, the U.S. theatrical year. In 1993 she was nominated for the Independent Spirit Award for Best Female Lead. She also appeared as D’orothea Wilson in the Channel 4/PBS miniseries Tales of the City, and as Rebecca in the Civil War picture released under titles including Ghost Brigade, Grey Knight, and The Killing Box. Encyclopedia.com says she hosted the syndicated Miss Collegiate African American Pageant in 1993.

    Wikipedia Encyclopedia.com IMDb AllMovie

  6. 1994–1998 — Film and television, mid-1990s

    Credits from this stretch, as listed by Wikipedia, TMDB, AllMovie, and Encyclopedia.com, include the short Wet (later the “Wet” segment of Tales of Erotica), Peace and Quiet (listed by Encyclopedia.com, TMDB, and AllMovie, but not on the Wikipedia film table), Condition Red, The Tie That Binds, The Sweeper, Spirit Lost, Black Rose of Harlem, Caught Up, Relax… It’s Just Sex, and The Last Call (also listed as Stingers). Television includes the “Fearless” episode of Showtime’s Fallen Angels, UPN’s Marker, the Showtime film Gang in Blue, and the “Hubris” episode of Fox’s New York Undercover. Some of those years conflict by a year across databases; the individual pages note the differences.

    Wikipedia Encyclopedia.com TMDB AllMovie

  7. 1998–2000 — The Wedding and Introducing Dorothy Dandridge

    She played Halle Berry’s screen sister in both ABC’s The Wedding (Oprah Winfrey Presents, 1998) and HBO’s Introducing Dorothy Dandridge (1999), a pairing also noted on her IMDb biography. Role-name spellings for The Wedding differ: Wikipedia and AllMovie list Liz Odis; Encyclopedia.com lists Liz Coles Otis. She also appeared in Lifetime’s The Courage to Love (2000) and BET’s Hidden Blessings (2000).

    Wikipedia Encyclopedia.com IMDb

  8. 2001–2015 — Continuing film and television work

    Sourced credits in this period include MacArthur Park, March, Showtime’s Violation, With or Without You, the short Shooter, When Do We Eat?, Our House, Frankie D, Divine Intervention, Tru Loved, Beautiful Loser, the documentary Reel Black Love (AllMovie lists a 2012 self credit; IMDb dates the title to 2011), Turning Point, Gangland (TMDB also lists the title Temporary File), and 72 Hours. Release years for a few of these titles differ by a year from one database to another.

    Wikipedia AllMovie TMDB

  9. 2016 — Pink Pantie Confessions

    220 Publishing released her book Pink Pantie Confessions, an anthology of memoirs, poetry, and insights, on January 25, 2016. In a quote published by her publisher, Williams said one of her goals had been to write her own book. A 2020 GO BANG! Magazine interview also describes a related blog and web series under the same title.

    220 Publishing Amazon GO BANG! Magazine, March 25, 2020

  10. 2017–2023 — Later film and television credits

    Wikipedia, TMDB, and AllMovie list The Hills, Ray Meets Helen, A Chance in the World (Wikipedia notes the on-screen credit as “Lynda Williams”), the shorts Wilted Rose, A Sisterhood of Signatures, Welfare Check (also producer), and Finding Dawn, plus Pieces of David, Every 21 Seconds, Everyday But Christmas, Why Me (also producer), The Misadventures of Mistress Maneater, Mad As Hell, Untold: The Back & Forth Story, Life Ain’t Like the Movies, the 2019 title Black Privilege (TMDB: Black & Privileged: Volume 1), and Kings of L.A. (2023). In the 2020 GO BANG! interview, Williams said she also developed the script for A Sisterhood of Signatures.

    Wikipedia AllMovie TMDB GO BANG! Magazine, March 25, 2020

  11. Stage work — years and venues still a gap

    In that March 25, 2020 interview, Williams said she had appeared on stage in Dreamgirls, The Talented Tenth, Langston Is My Man, Hope Runs Eternal, Twilight in Los Angeles, 1992, Make Em’ Laugh, Make Em’ Laugh: A Christmas Cabaret, and Immediate Family. The interview did not give years, theaters, or roles. Those details remain gaps. The same interview says she and Thea Camara taught acting workshops as “Acting Up with Thea and Cynda.” Whether that workshop is still running has not been independently confirmed here.

    GO BANG! Magazine, March 25, 2020

  12. Podcast, Live & Direct, and books — 2020s

    She hosts the P.P.C. Podcast with Cynda Williams, whose Spotify for Creators page identifies her as the actress from Mo’ Better Blues and lists episodes into 2022. In 2021 she contributed to Faith, Failure, Success Vol. 2: Surviving the Storm (220 Publishing). A 2024 documentary of the same series, Faith, Failure, Success, lists her as herself (IMDbPro; the producer announced a November 15, 2024 debut). She hosts “Live & Direct with Cyn and Friends” on Instagram Live, Thursdays at 5 p.m. Central, at @cyndawill — an account confirmed as hers by her LinkedIn posts and by collaborator Facey Creative.

    P.P.C. Podcast Amazon (B09NGJSBSV) 220 Publishing Facey Creative LinkedIn

  13. Where she is now — 2026, still in progress

    Her LinkedIn profile, under Cindy Williams-Plummer, professional name Cynda Williams, lists her as CEO of CWILLCreate Productions LLC (from December 2023) and says she is finishing the music for a television series she wrote, and continues to act, sing, and write. A LinkedIn post dated November 9, 2026, said one of her social media pages had been hacked and then shut down by the platform; the post did not name the platform. AllMovie lists two 2025 titles, The Midas Code (Auntie Denise) and Surviving Entertainment (self); those were not independently confirmed with press for this build. IMDb also listed additional projects in pre-production or filming; those remain unconfirmed here.

    LinkedIn AllMovie IMDb
