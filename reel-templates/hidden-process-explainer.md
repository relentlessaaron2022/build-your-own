## The template (fill in the brackets)

### 1. The Flip — 0 to 5 seconds
> "When you **[ordinary moment everybody has]**, your **[mind / body / life]** doesn't **[what people assume]**. It **[what is really going on underneath]**."

- One sentence. No intro, no name, no "hey y'all."
- The picture has to be moving inward: a push-in, a close-up, something getting nearer.

### 2. The Lopsided Number — 5 to 12 seconds
> "**[Small thing]** is only **[X]**, but it controls **[Y]**."

- One real number from a real source. Write the source in your notes even if it doesn't go on screen.
- Put the number on screen in your accent color.

### 3. The Mess — 12 to 25 seconds
> "All that **[effort / pressure / living]** leaves something behind: **[the residue]**. And by **[time]**, it's been piling up for **[duration]**."

- Slow down here. This is the part where the viewer recognizes themselves.

### 4. The Riddle and the Name — 25 to 40 seconds
> "Most of the time, **[the normal fix]** handles it. But **[this situation]** doesn't have that. So it uses something else: **[the named thing]**."

- Give the process a name and put it on screen as a title card. A real term (from science, history, law, money) or a Relentless term you coin and keep using across videos. Coined terms build the brand; reuse them.

### 5. First, Next, Then — 40 seconds to about 1:45
> "Here's how it works. **First**, … **Next**, … **Then**, …"

- Three steps, sometimes four. Never more.
- Label every new part on screen with a short tag and a pointer line.
- Drop the serious stake (the thing that can go wrong if this process breaks) inside step 2 or 3, not at the start.

### 6. It Doesn't Stop — about 1:45 to 2:10
> "And this doesn't happen once. It happens **[how often]**, **[for how long]**. By **[later point]**, **[what's changed]**."

### 7. Send Them Home With It — last 10 seconds
> "So **[tonight / tomorrow / next time you X]**, remember: **[the reframe in plain words]**."

- No hard call to action. The reframe is the call to action.
- Slow pull-back. Warm color. Let the music land and stop.

## Production checklist

- **Length:** 1:30 to 2:30. The original runs 2:25 and keeps people because of the steps.
- **Voice:** calm, low, unhurried. Read it like you're explaining something to one person at a kitchen table at night.
- **Captions:** white, bottom center, dark outline, sentence case, timed line by line.
- **Labels:** one accent color (the original uses gold/yellow) for every on-screen term and number. Same color every video so the feed looks like one series.
- **Cuts:** one cut per idea. Cuts land on "First," "Next," "Then."
- **Sound:** soft ambient pad under everything. Small whoosh or chime when a number or label appears. A low pulse during the process section. A warm resolving chord at the end.
- **Visuals:** 3D or motion graphics if you have them; if you're filming yourself, swap the push-ins for slow camera moves toward your hands, a page, a window, a street, and keep the labels.
- **Cover frame:** the image at 0:00 should already show the topic with a 4–6 word title (original: "HOW YOUR BRAIN CLEANS DURING SLEEP").
- **Truth check before posting:** every number and every named process has a source you can point to. If a personal story is in it, it happened the way you tell it.

### Caption formula
> Line 1: the flip, restated in under 12 words.
> Line 2–3: the named process and the one fact people will repeat.
> Line 4: a question that asks them to look at their own life ("When did you last…?").

Hashtags: 5–8, mixing one broad (#mindset), two topic tags, and one series tag you own (e.g. #RelentlessExplains).

4. **Reinvention** — what neuroplasticity actually means after 30, 40, 50.
5. **Shame vs. guilt** — two different processes, two different outcomes.
6. **Compound interest** — the quiet process that runs every day whether you're watching or not.
7. **Grief** — what the mind is doing when it keeps "forgetting" someone is gone.

## Worked example — "You don't break a habit"

> **[0:00]** When you try to quit something, your brain doesn't delete the old habit. It keeps it. Filed away. Ready.
>
> **[0:06]** A habit runs on a small cluster deep in your brain called the basal ganglia. It's tiny. But it can run a whole routine while the rest of you is somewhere else.
>
> **[0:12]** Every time you repeat something, that routine gets packed tighter. Cue. Action. Reward. Over and over, until you don't even feel yourself choosing it.
>
> **[0:26]** You'd think willpower handles that. It doesn't, not for long. Willpower lives up front, and it gets tired. So the brain leans on something older. Researchers call it **chunking**.
>
> **[0:40]** Here's how it works. **First**, a cue shows up. A time. A place. A feeling you don't want to sit with.
> **Next**, the chunk fires. The whole routine, start to finish, like one move.
> **Then** the reward lands, and the loop gets a little stronger.
> That's why the old habit comes back on a bad day. It was never gone. It was waiting for its cue.
>
> **[1:45]** So you don't fight the chunk. You build a new one on the same cue. Same time, same place, same feeling, different action. In one well-known study, new habits took anywhere from 18 days to 254. Most people landed around 66.
>
> **[2:10]** So tomorrow, when the old pull shows up, don't call it failure. Call it a cue. And give it somewhere new to go.
