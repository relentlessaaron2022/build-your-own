A life in progress

This timeline is in progress. Every entry below is tied to a public source. Dates, venues, and early credits that we could not verify are marked as gaps — they are not filled in.

  1. Childhood — Bronx, New York

    Bronx-born. Black America Web (July 8, 2014) describes him as a class clown who impersonated teachers on the school PA. A promotional biography circulated on IMDb and by Atlanta Comedy Theatre says he was the eldest of four, raised by a single mom, and later attended Dewitt Clinton High School, where he lettered in football, basketball, and boxing and was All City in football.

    source source source

  2. After high school — Navy, then writing jokes at night

    The same promotional biography says that after high school he enlisted in the Navy, later worked security, and wrote jokes at night. Those early work dates are not independently confirmed here, so they stay labeled as coming from that bio.

    source

  3. Television, film, and comedy specials — dates still being pinned down

    Black America Web, a 2014 PR Newswire release, and Comedy House all credit appearances on HBO’s Def Comedy Jam, Showtime at the Apollo, Conan O’Brien, and BET’s Comic View. Comedy House also lists NBC’s Last Comic Standing Season 8, Comedy Central, and Chocolate Sundaes (hosted by Tommy Davidson). Films listed there: The Other Brother, Death of a Dynasty, Cookout 2, Budz House, and Budz House Backstage. DVDs listed: Comedy in The Hood, Laugh Bandits No. 1, Gerald Kelly Live in New York, Gospel Comedy All-Stars 1 & 2, and Gerald Kelly on Broadway. Exact air dates for the TV spots were not found for this build.

    source source source

  4. Radio

    Comedy House says he was a morning-show co-host on 107.5 WBLS in New York and part of the morning team at Hot 93.7 WZMX in Hartford, Connecticut, and that his characters Grandma and Tahata were New England favorites. Those radio dates were not independently confirmed here.

    source

  5. December 8, 2013 — YouTube channel created

    His official YouTube channel, now at @Geraldkellycomedy, was created on December 8, 2013 (YouTube channel feed). The about text on the channel: “Experience the comedic genius of Gerald Kelly with these hilarious clips, skits and interviews!!!”

    source source

  6. November 4, 2013 — Tom Joyner Morning Show

    Black America Web reported that the Tom Joyner Morning Show talked with him about the Microphones Up, Guns Down initiative, and posted audio from the appearance.

    source

  7. 2013–2014 — Microphones Up, Guns Down

    Gerald and Isiah Kelly’s Microphones Up, Guns Down initiative was covered by PIX11 (they were also performing at Carolines on Broadway) and, in July 2014, by Black America Web and BroadwayWorld. Kelly described traveling with his wife and sons to schools, YMCAs, and neighborhoods to teach kids to use a microphone instead of a gun, including a role-play segment called “What Are You Looking At?” His X profile still identifies him as CEO of Microphones Up Guns Down.

    source source source source

  8. July 30, 2014 — Punchline Comedy Club, Atlanta

    Black America Web reported he would be at Punchline Comedy Club in Atlanta on July 30, 2014.

    source

  9. August 26, 2014 — Live On Broadway DVD

    Novus Content released “Gerald Kelly Live On Broadway,” filmed at the Beacon Theater in New York and directed by Leslie Smalls, according to PR Newswire (August 20, 2014). The release also quotes Russell Simmons calling him “the funniest comedian in the country.” Comedy House separately quotes Simmons calling him the “Black Jackie Gleason.”

    source source

  10. 2021 — America’s Got Talent, with Lil Hunter Kelly

    A 2021 America’s Got Talent Facebook post describes Gerald Kelly and his son Lil Hunter Kelly taking the stage together. Later event listings (including a Park Theatre post) billed him as “as seen on America’s Got Talent.” We have not yet attached an official NBC clip to this entry.

    source source

  11. November 7, 2023 — “We Different,” HITKOR

    HITKOR posted a live exclusive of his bit “We Different” from Comedy Overload, hosted by Lavell Crawford.

    source

  12. 2025–2026 — Official YouTube clips, including Who’s Raising Who

    His official channel has been posting stand-up and family comedy shorts, including “Who’s Raising Who” clips that tag @lilhunterkelly, plus bits such as “My thoughts on funerals” and “GOT TO GET IN SHAPE NOWWW.”

    source

  13. May 4, 2026 — Gerald Kelly’s Street Stories Volume 1

    Zero Chill Records released the album Gerald Kelly’s Street Stories Volume 1 (UPC 825293943632). Writers are credited as Gerald Kelly and Relentless Aaron; composers as DeWitt T Gilmore and Relentless Aaron. The title track “Mics Up, Guns Down” was provided to YouTube by DistroKid and is on the Gerald Kelly - Topic channel. RelentlessAaron.net published “Street Stories with Gerald Kelly: The Unfiltered Voice of the City” on May 17, 2026.

    source source source
