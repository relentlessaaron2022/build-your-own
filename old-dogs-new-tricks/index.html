Put the Email in the Email Box

Miss Pearl opens with a mix up she meets all the time. One person types an email address into the box that is asking for a website. Another person drops a whole website address into the box that is asking for an email. Both boxes refuse, nobody explains why, and the person at the keyboard ends up cross with a machine. Pearl slows the whole thing down, sorts the two addresses out in plain words, and shows you the one key to press when your screen starts shouting at you in capital letters.

Then comes the gem of the week. The phone you already own, the one you argue with, can read small print aloud, remind you when it is tablet time, put your grandbabies on the screen from miles away, and even help you earn a little money from what you already know.

Three jewels to keep

Jewel one

One brings letters. One is a place.

Your email address is where messages come to you, like a mailbox at your own gate. A website address is a place you go to visit, like a shop you walk into. Letters go in the mailbox. Feet go through the shop door. Keep each address in its own box and half the fuss on the internet goes quiet.

Jewel two

If the screen shouts, check one key.

When every letter you type comes out in capitals, the computer is not vexed with you. The caps lock key, over on the left of the keyboard, got pressed by accident. Press it once more and the shouting stops. The machine only ever does what it was told.

Jewel three

Your knowledge is worth money.

The recipes you cook from memory, the stories only you can tell, the skills your hands learned over thirty years: free AI tools can help you write all of it down plain and tidy. You did the learning over a lifetime. The tool just does the typing, fast, and never gets tired of your questions.
