Welcome to The GitHub Report, Money Series. Number one. One repo, one way it pays, and the one line you cannot cross.
Today's repo is OpenClaw. It is the fastest climb GitHub has ever seen. By March, it had passed 250,000 stars, and it climbed past Linux on the all-time board.
So what is it? OpenClaw is a personal AI assistant you run on your own machine. It answers on the messaging apps people already use. It can read email, keep a calendar, open files, work a browser, and run commands. And it does not clock out. It runs all day and all night.
Here is the money. Every small business you walk past has the same leak. Messages come in after hours, and nobody answers. The barber is cutting hair. The contractor is on a roof. The salon owner is doing a client's color. Those messages turn into customers for somebody else.
OpenClaw can answer them. It can book the appointment on the calendar, send the reminder the day before, and chase the invoice that is thirty days late. Then it sends the owner a short summary every morning.
The software is free. Your setup is not. That is the business. You are not selling code. You are selling a working assistant, set up, tested, and kept running.
Here is a starting price I would use. Five hundred dollars to set it up. One hundred fifty a month to keep it updated and watched. Five clients on a monthly plan is seven hundred fifty dollars a month before you set up anybody new. These are my numbers, not a promise. Your market sets the price.
Now, what it costs you. It needs a computer that stays on. A spare desktop works, or a small cloud server. It needs an AI model to think with, and that model has a bill. Pass that bill to the client, line by line. And it costs you time. Plan on a weekend for your first install, and a few hours for every one after that.
Now, the license line. The project's page shows the MIT license, which lets you sell your setup work. One tracking site lists the license as other, so open the LICENSE file and read it yourself before you take money. And every add-on skill you install has its own license. Check each one.
And one more line, because this one can hurt somebody. Security experts have warned that agents like this can be risky in a business. It can read email and run commands. So give it only the accounts it needs. Never hand it banking passwords. And keep a human approving anything that moves money. Your reputation is the product.
Here is your first client. Pick one business you already go to. Your barber. Your mechanic. The salon down the street. Send this: I set up AI assistants that answer your customers after hours and book them on your calendar. Let me run one for your shop free for two weeks. If it books you one extra customer, we talk price.
One yes is a business. Two weeks of proof is a sales pitch you never have to write again.
