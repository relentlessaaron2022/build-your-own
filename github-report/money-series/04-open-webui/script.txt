Welcome to The GitHub Report, Money Series. Number four. One repo, one way it pays, and the one line you cannot cross.
Today's repo is Open WebUI. More than 140,000 stars. If Ollama from number three is the engine, Open WebUI is the dashboard.
So what is it? Open WebUI is a chat screen for AI that you host yourself. It looks and feels like the big AI apps. It works with local models through Ollama, and with cloud models too. Your team gets logins, shared prompts, and the ability to chat with their own documents. And it all runs on a server you control.
Here is the money. Small companies want AI for their whole team, but they do not want ten different accounts, ten different bills, and nobody watching what goes where. The owner wants one front door.
You build that front door. One company portal. Every employee logs in. The sales team gets the prompts that write proposals. The office manager gets the prompt that turns notes into an email. The owner decides which models anybody can use, and every file stays on the company's own server.
You are not selling a chatbot. You are selling one front door for a whole team.
Here is a starting price I would use. Seven hundred fifty dollars to set it up, with the logins, the starter prompts, and a training session. One hundred fifty dollars a month to keep it updated and add new prompts. Six companies is nine hundred dollars a month. These are my numbers, not a promise. Your market sets the price.
Now, what it costs you. It needs a server. A small cloud server runs it fine if the models live in the cloud. If the client wants fully private AI, pair it with an Ollama machine, like we covered in number three. Cloud models send a usage bill, so pass it through. And it costs you time, because the real value is the prompts you build for each team.
Now, the license line, and this one changed. Open WebUI used to be under a plain BSD license. Starting with version zero point six point six, the license adds a branding rule. In most cases, you may not remove or change the Open WebUI name and logo. The license lists its own exceptions. Read the current LICENSE file before you sell anything. The simple rule is this. Do not sell Open WebUI as your own product with your own name on it. Sell your setup, your prompts, and your support, with their name left on.
And one more line. A team portal holds the company's files and the company's questions. Turn on user roles from day one. Not everybody needs admin. Write down who can see what, and hand the owner the keys, so the system belongs to them and not to you.
Here is your first client. Pick a small company with five to twenty people. A real estate office. A marketing agency. A dental group. Send this: I set up a private AI portal for your whole team, with one login each and your files staying on your own server. Let me run a one-hour demo with your team on a test portal. If they use it, we talk price.
The test portal is the pitch. Once a team has its own prompts with its own company name in them, they do not want to go back.
