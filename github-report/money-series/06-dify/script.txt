Today's repo is Dify. More than 130,000 stars. It is one of the most starred platforms for building AI apps on GitHub.
So what is it? Dify lets you build AI apps without writing much code. Chatbots, assistants, and workflows, built on a visual canvas. You can feed it a business's own documents, so the bot answers from their real information. It works with many different AI models, and you can host it yourself.
Here is the money. Every business answers the same twenty questions every day. What are your hours. Do you take my insurance. How much is shipping. Can I reschedule. The owner answers on the phone, in the inbox, and in the comments, over and over.
You can build them a support assistant that knows their business. Feed Dify their price list, their policies, and their FAQ. Put the chat on their website. It answers the twenty questions at midnight, and when a question is too hard, it hands the customer to a human.
You are not selling a chatbot. You are selling the twenty questions answered, so the owner can do the work only they can do.
Here is a starting price I would use. One thousand dollars to build and train the assistant on their documents. Two hundred fifty dollars a month to keep it updated when their prices and policies change. Four clients is a thousand dollars a month. These are my numbers, not a promise. Your market sets the price.
Now, what it costs you. Self-hosted Dify needs a server with a bit more muscle than the last few tools. The AI model sends a usage bill, and a busy website means a bigger bill, so build it into the monthly price. And it costs you time, because the answers are only as good as the documents you feed it.
Now, the license line, and read this one twice. Dify uses the Apache license with extra conditions added. Two of them matter for you. First, you may not use the Dify code to run a multi-tenant service, meaning one platform serving many customers, unless Dify gives you written permission. Second, you may not remove or change the Dify logo and copyright in the console. Some people argue that makes Dify source available rather than truly open source. So build each client their own setup, on their own account or server, and leave the logo alone. If you want to run one platform for many clients, contact Dify for a commercial license first.
And one more line. A support bot speaks for the business. If it makes up a refund policy, the business owns that mistake. Tell the bot to answer only from the documents, test it with the hardest questions you can think of, and always give the customer a way to reach a real person.
Here is your first client. Pick a business that gets the same questions all day. A dental office. A boutique. A moving company. Send this: I build AI assistants that answer your customers' common questions on your website, twenty four seven, using only your own information. Send me your FAQ and I will build you a free test version this week. If it answers like you would, we talk price.
The test version sells itself. When the owner asks it a question and hears their own policy come back, you have a client.
