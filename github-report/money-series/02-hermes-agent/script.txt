Today's repo is Hermes Agent, from Nous Research. It went public on February 25th of this year. By summer, it had passed 200,000 stars.
So what is it? Hermes is an AI agent that grows with you. It remembers. It keeps a memory of your work, and it writes its own skills as it learns what you need. It talks to you where you already are. Telegram, Discord, Slack. It works with almost any AI model. And it can run on your own machine or your own server, so the memory stays with you.
Here is the money. Busy professionals are drowning in follow-up. The real estate agent with forty open leads. The insurance broker with renewals every week. The consultant who promised a proposal by Friday and forgot. They do not need another app. They need a chief of staff who never forgets.
Hermes can be that chief of staff. It remembers every client and every promise. It drafts the follow-up in Slack before the deadline. It pulls the notes together before the meeting. And every week it learns a little more about how that person works.
You are not selling software. You are selling a chief of staff that lives on their own machine.
Here is a starting price I would use. Three hundred dollars to set it up and teach it the first month of their work. One hundred dollars a month to keep it tuned. Ten clients is a thousand dollars a month, and the setups pay for your time up front. These are my numbers, not a promise. Your market sets the price.
Now, what it costs you. It needs a machine or a small server that stays on. It needs an AI model, and a cloud model sends a bill every month. Pass that bill through. And it costs you time. The first month matters most, because that is when it learns the person.
Now, the license line. Hermes Agent is MIT licensed. You can install it for clients and charge for the work. But the model is a separate thing. Each model has its own license and its own terms. Read the model's license before you build a business on it.
And one more line, because this one is about trust. Hermes keeps a memory. That memory holds client names, deals, and private notes. If you use a cloud model, those words leave the machine every time it thinks. So tell your client exactly where their data goes. Put it in writing. And when a client leaves, delete the memory and show them you did.
