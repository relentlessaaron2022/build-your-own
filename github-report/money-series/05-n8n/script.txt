Welcome to The GitHub Report, Money Series. Number five. One repo, one way it pays, and the one line you cannot cross.
Today's repo is n8n. More than 200,000 stars. Last year it gained more stars than any other JavaScript project on GitHub.
So what is it? n8n is a workflow automation tool. You connect apps together on a canvas, box to box, and the work moves on its own. A form comes in, a contact goes into the CRM, a text goes out, and a task lands on somebody's list. It has hundreds of connections, AI steps built in, and you can host it yourself.
Here is the money. Every small business does the same boring tasks every single day. Somebody copies the lead from the website into a spreadsheet. Somebody sends the same follow-up email by hand. Somebody reminds the customer about the unpaid invoice, again. Those hours are expensive, and nobody loves doing them.
You can sell automations. One workflow at a time. New lead comes in, it gets added to the CRM, it gets a text in under a minute, and the owner gets a ping. Invoice is seven days late, a polite reminder goes out on its own. The owner gets their hours back, and you get paid for building the machine.
You are not selling software. You are selling hours back.
Here is a starting price I would use. Five hundred dollars per workflow you build and test. Two hundred dollars a month to keep them running and fix them when an app changes. Four clients on a monthly plan is eight hundred dollars a month, before any new builds. These are my numbers, not a promise. Your market sets the price.
Now, what it costs you. Self-hosted n8n needs a small server, and some of the apps you connect have their own fees. AI steps send a model bill. Pass every one of those through. And it costs you time, because the real work is learning how the business actually runs before you automate it.
Now, the license line, and this one is important. n8n is not under a typical open source license. It uses the Sustainable Use License. In plain words, you can build workflows for a client, especially on the client's own n8n. What you cannot do is host n8n as a service where your clients log in and build their own workflows, or resell n8n with your name on it. Running many clients' automations on your one server is a gray area. If your business model looks like that, read the license FAQ and email n8n before you scale. They sell commercial licenses for exactly that.
And one more line. Automations run when nobody is watching. Test every workflow with fake data first. Build in an alert so the owner knows when something breaks. And never let an automation send money or delete records without a human saying yes.
Here is your first client. Pick a business that gets leads online. A roofer. A tutor. A wedding photographer. Send this: I build automations that answer your new leads in under a minute and chase late invoices for you. Tell me the one task you hate doing every week, and I will automate it free. If you like it, we talk about the rest.
One free workflow is your proof. When the owner stops doing the task they hate, they will ask what else you can automate.
