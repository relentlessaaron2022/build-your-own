Today's repo is Ollama. More than 170,000 stars. MIT licensed. It is one of the most starred AI projects on all of GitHub.
So what is it? Ollama runs AI models on your own computer. Mac, Windows, or Linux. You download a model with one command, and it answers questions, writes, and summarizes, without sending a single word to the cloud. No account. No monthly AI bill. Once the model is downloaded, it can run with the internet turned off.
Here is the money. Think about the offices that are scared of AI. The law office. The accountant during tax season. The small medical practice. They have heard the stories about private files getting pasted into chatbots. So their staff either uses AI in secret, or not at all.
You can sell them a private assistant that lives in their office. A computer in the back room running Ollama. Their staff asks it to summarize a contract, draft a letter, or clean up meeting notes, and the words never leave the building.
You are not selling AI. You are selling peace of mind with AI inside it.
Here is a starting price I would use. One thousand dollars to set it up, plus the computer at cost. Two hundred dollars a month to keep it updated and to train the staff. Four offices is eight hundred dollars a month, and every setup fee pays for your first months of work. These are my numbers, not a promise. Your market sets the price.
Now, what it costs you. Local AI needs memory. A strong desktop with plenty of RAM, or a Mac with a lot of unified memory, runs the mid-size models well. Small models run on less. Test the exact model on the exact machine before you quote a price. And it costs you time, because the real work is training the staff to use it.
Now, the license line. Ollama itself is MIT licensed, so you can install it for clients and charge for your work. But every model you download has its own license. Some allow commercial use freely. Some have conditions and limits. Read the license of each model you put in an office, and keep a copy in the client's file.
And one more line, because this one is about the law. Private is not the same as compliant. A medical office and a law firm have rules about client data. Running AI locally helps, but it does not make anybody compliant by itself. Do not promise compliance. Tell them to check with their own lawyer or compliance person, and put that in your contract.
