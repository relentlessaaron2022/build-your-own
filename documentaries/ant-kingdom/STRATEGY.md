# When the Little Ones Come Together: content strategy

**The idea in one line:** *One ant is small. A thousand ants are a force.*
**Why it works for Relentless:** it's a nature story that is really a story about people: underdogs, families, communities, the self-made. It carries your voice the way the Be Nice and Daily Message pieces do, and it works with wonder alone (no politics, no outrage).

## 3. The documentary: *The Ant Kingdom*

**Built with:** the Cinematic Skill (installed in this project): one continuous camera gliding over a board of photos, maps, documents and big numbers, every hit landing on the narrator's word. **Look:** `night` (dark board, warm light), with archive and nature photos from Wikimedia Commons, every one credited. **Voice:** yours (recommended for this one; it's personal) or Stormi's. **Cost:** $0.

### Story shape (about 1,400 words of narration ≈ 9 minutes)

**Cold open (0:00–0:45): the giant falls.**
The cockroach scene, told in words over a single still. *"A giant, by any measure… and it never stood a chance."* Title card: WHEN THE LITTLE ONES COME TOGETHER.

**Act 1 (0:45–2:00): the numbers.**
- About **20 quadrillion ants** on Earth, a conservative estimate from 489 studies across every continent (Schultheiss et al., *PNAS*, 2022).
- That's roughly **2.5 million ants for every person alive.**
- Their combined weight: about 12 million tonnes of dry carbon, more than all wild birds and wild mammals put together (to be confirmed against the paper before narration).
- Big-number cards, counting up.

**Act 2 (2:00–3:45): the secret. No one is in charge.**
- The queen doesn't give orders. She lays eggs.
- Pheromones: a chemical language. A trail to food gets stronger every time another ant walks it.
- Mandibles: grip, bite, cut, carry.
- "Collective behavior": each ant follows local signals; the colony solves problems no ant understands.
- Visual: a map-style trail that thickens as "ants" (dots) recruit.

**Act 3 (3:45–7:30): four case files.** Each is a cluster on the board, stamped like an evidence file.
1. **The army that never stops:** army ants and their raids.
2. **The first farmers:** leafcutter ants grow fungus gardens, millions of years before human agriculture.
3. **The living raft:** fire ants link bodies to float through floods.
4. **The empire:** an Argentine ant supercolony stretches about **6,000 km** along the coasts of southern Europe, from Italy to Spain's Atlantic coast. Ants from it don't fight each other; ants from a rival supercolony fight to the death (Giraud, Pedersen & Keller, 2002). The biggest "creature" in Europe is made of little ones.

**Act 4 (7:30–8:45): the lesson.**
Communities, families, businesses, movements. *"The biggest creature doesn't always win. The strongest individual doesn't always survive."* Your closing lines, as written.

**Close (8:45–9:15):** *"One ant is small. A thousand ants are a force."* RELENTLESS AARON. Newsletter + subscribe.

### Fact check before narration (the skill's first approval gate)
Each line gets a source note. Already confirmed:

## 5. LinkedIn: the leadership angle

> **No ant understands the whole mission. The colony still wins.**
> Ants don't have managers. They have signals. A trail gets stronger every time someone uses it; a dead end fades. That's how 20 quadrillion of them run the planet without a meeting.
> Three lessons I'm taking into my own work:
> 1. Make the signal clear (everyone should know what "food" and "danger" look like).
> 2. Reward the trail that works (reinforce what's proven; let the rest fade).
> 3. Small and coordinated beats big and alone.
> The biggest company in the room doesn't always win. The most coordinated one usually does.
