# Built Different, Case 009: The 17-Minute Breath (verified notes)

Only facts below appear in the script. Where sources disagree, the script takes the safe side or leaves it out.

| Claim used in script | Source |
|---|---|
| April 30, 2008, live on The Oprah Winfrey Show, Blaine held his breath 17 min 4 s underwater in a water-filled sphere; a Guinness judge certified it | Live Science; NPR (2008-05-01); Fox News/AP |
| He breathed pure oxygen beforehand (about 23 minutes); Guinness allowed up to 30 | Live Science; Fox News/AP |
| On the show, monitors showed his heart slowing and extra beats | Oprah.com, "Can David Blaine Do It?" |
| The record fell months later: Tom Sietas, 17 min 19 s, Sept 2008 | Live Science / Guinness reporting |
| Today's oxygen-assisted record: Vitomir Maričić, 29 min 3 s (2025) | Guinness World Records news, Sept 2025 |
| 2006 "Drowned Alive": a week living in an 8-ft water sphere at Lincoln Center, then a breath-hold attempt in chains; divers pulled him out at 7 min 8 s after his body began convulsing | CBS News; NY Sun; ABC News; China Daily/AP |
| By his own account: lost 50+ lb in about three months; resting heart rate fell to 38 bpm | NPR TED Radio Hour transcript (2014); TEDMED 2009 summaries (his claim, not a measurement) |
| Diving response: breath stops, heart slows, blood vessels in the limbs tighten, spleen contracts and releases red blood cells | Panneton 2013, Physiology (PMC3768097); Frontiers in Physiology 2019 review |
| The urge to breathe is driven mainly by rising CO2, not falling oxygen | DAN Alert Diver, "Hypoxia in Breath-Hold Diving"; Red Cross |
| Hyperventilating before a breath-hold can cause blackout with no warning; never do it before going underwater | DAN; Royal Life Saving Australia; Red Cross |

Left out: the exact record he chased in 2006 (sources give different numbers), "arrhythmia" as a diagnosis, his oxygen-free personal best (no solid source found).
