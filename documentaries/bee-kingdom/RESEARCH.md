# Bees: research notes (verified, with sources)

Only facts below make it into the script. Anything we couldn't confirm is listed at the bottom and stays out.

## Confirmed
- **20,000+ bee species**, and most pollinators are wild (FAO, *Why bees matter*). https://www.unccd.int/sites/default/files/library/1490_Why_bees_matter_FAO_i9527en.pdf
- **Pollinators support 87 of the leading food crops** and affect 35% of global agricultural land (FAO). UNEP, citing FAO bee experts: about **a third of the world's food production depends on bees**. https://www.fao.org/pollination/about · https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/story/why-bees-are-essential-people-and-planet
- **The waggle dance:** a returning forager dances to tell nestmates where food is. The angle of the waggle run gives the direction relative to the sun; the duration gives the distance. Decoded by **Karl von Frisch**, who shared the **1973 Nobel Prize** in Physiology or Medicine. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_von_Frisch · https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/frisch-lecture.pdf
- **Honeybee democracy (Thomas Seeley, Cornell):** when a swarm needs a new home, scouts inspect sites and dance for them; a scout dances longer for a better site and honestly less for a mediocre one. The swarm decides by **quorum**: when about 15 scouts are at one site at the same time, the swarm commits. Book: *Honeybee Democracy* (Princeton, 2010). https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2006/04/honeybee-decision-making-ability-rivals-any-department-committee · https://www.americanscientist.org/article/evolution-religion-and-free-will
- **The heat ball:** Japanese honeybees mob an attacking giant hornet with **500+ bees**, forming a ball that heats to about **47 °C** (Ono et al., *Nature* 1995). Later work shows the ball also traps CO₂, which lowers the hornet's heat limit to about 45–46 °C, while the bees survive up to about 50 °C. https://www.nature.com/articles/377334a0 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22233493/
- **Colony and life:** a colony can hold up to ~60,000 workers in summer (Univ. of Delaware MAAREC); a summer worker lives about **six weeks** (Delaware; Mississippi State: 5–7 weeks). The colony holds its nest near **93 °F (~34 °C)** on hot days by spreading water that evaporates. https://canr.udel.edu/maarec/honey-bee-biology/the-colony-and-its-organization/ · https://canr.udel.edu/maarec/honey-bee-biology/seasonal-cycles-of-activities-in-colonies/
