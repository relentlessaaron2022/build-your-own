Case number zero zero seven. Subject: the honeybee. Charge: keeping the world fed.
This is the file.
Exhibit one. The numbers.
There are more than twenty thousand species of bees. Most of them are wild, and most of them you have never met.
And according to United Nations food experts, about a third of the world's food production depends on bees.
One in every three bites. Think about that the next time you eat.
Exhibit two. The dance.
A worker finds flowers. She flies home, and she dances.
Her body points the way, measured against the sun. The longer she waggles, the farther the flowers are.
The other bees read it, and they fly straight to the food.
A scientist named Karl von Frisch decoded that dance. In 1973, it helped earn him a Nobel Prize.
The bees had been speaking the whole time. We just had to learn the language.
Exhibit three. The vote.
When a colony outgrows its home, thousands of bees leave with the old queen and hang together in a cluster.
They need a new home, fast. So scouts go out and inspect the options.
Each scout comes back and dances for the place she found. A great site gets a long dance. A poor site gets a short one. She does not oversell.
Other scouts go and check her work.
And when about fifteen scouts are standing at the same site at the same time, the decision is made. The whole swarm lifts off and moves.
No king. No campaign money. Just honest reports, and a quorum.
Exhibit four. The heat ball.
In Japan, a giant hornet can wipe out a hive. So the Japanese honeybee has a defense.
Five hundred bees or more surround the hornet in a tight ball, and they vibrate their flight muscles.
The ball heats to about forty seven degrees Celsius. Hot enough to kill the hornet. Not hot enough to kill the bees.
No single bee can beat that hornet. Five hundred together cook it.
Exhibit five. The colony.
In summer, a colony can hold sixty thousand workers. A worker lives about six weeks.
They keep the nursery near ninety three degrees on hot days, cooling it with water they spread and fan dry.
Six weeks. That is all the time one bee gets. And the colony outlives every one of them.
The verdict.
On the charge of keeping the world fed, the honeybee is found essential.
Not because one bee is strong. Because none of them works alone.
Case closed. Produced by Relentless Aaron.
