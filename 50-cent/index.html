A G-Unit Books novel from Gallery Books / Simon & Schuster, co-credited to Relentless Aaron and 50 Cent. Publisher page: July 24, 2007, 256 pages, ISBN 9781416549499. Amazon lists July 12, 2007 for the same edition. The official blurb follows Jamel Ross after seven years in federal prison; one library catalog disagrees on the character name and page count. A life in progress

This timeline is in progress. Every entry is tied to a public source. Dates, credits, and deals we could not verify are marked as gaps — they are not filled in. Private family details, unsourced relationship gossip, and feud blow-by-blows are left off.

  1. 1975 — South Jamaica, Queens

    Curtis James Jackson III was born July 6, 1975, in the South Jamaica neighborhood of Queens, New York City, according to Wikipedia’s biography (citing published profiles). That date is the one used across standard film and music databases linked from this hub.

    Wikipedia

  2. 1996–2000 — First recordings, and a canceled debut

    Wikipedia says he began pursuing a musical career in 1996, and that in 1999–2000 he recorded a debut album, Power of the Dollar, for Columbia Records. In May 2000 he was struck by nine bullets; the album’s release was canceled and he was dropped from the label. The unreleased/shelved project is listed on the albums discography and has its own page in this hub. Street-level details beyond that sourced summary are left off.

    Wikipedia Albums discography

  3. 2002 — Mixtapes and a Shady / Aftermath / Interscope deal

    The 2002 mixtape Guess Who’s Back?, Wikipedia says, was discovered by Eminem, who signed Jackson to Shady Records, an Interscope imprint, that year — in the Aftermath system associated with Dr. Dre. Early G-Unit mixtapes from the same period, including 50 Cent Is the Future, No Mercy, No Fear, and God’s Plan, are listed on both his albums discography and the G-Unit discography.

    Wikipedia Albums discography

  4. 2003 — Get Rich or Die Tryin’ and G-Unit Records

    His debut studio album, Get Rich or Die Tryin’, was released February 6, 2003 (U.S.), per the albums discography (citing AllMusic). Wikipedia says it reached No. 1 on the Billboard 200, was certified multi-platinum by the RIAA, and produced the Billboard Hot 100 No. 1 singles “In da Club” and “21 Questions” (the latter featuring Nate Dogg). The same year, Wikipedia says, he launched G-Unit Records. A Hip-Hop Wired venture timeline also dates a G-Unit Clothing partnership with Marc Ecko and a Reebok footwear deal to 2003, and G-Unit Films to the same year.

    Wikipedia Albums discography Hip-Hop Wired

  5. 2005 — The Massacre, a feature film, and Sirius radio

    Wikipedia records The Massacre as his second studio album, another Billboard 200 No. 1, with “Candy Shop” (featuring Olivia) as a third Hot 100 No. 1. He starred as Marcus “Young Caesar” Greer in the 2005 film Get Rich or Die Tryin’ (the main Wikipedia filmography and the videography page agree on the film; role wording is slightly fuller on the main page). CNN/Money reported on February 24, 2005, that he would host G-Unit Radio on Sirius’s Shade 45, co-hosted by DJ Whoo Kid. A video game, 50 Cent: Bulletproof, is listed for 2005 on both filmography tables.

    Wikipedia CNN/Money, Feb. 24, 2005

  6. 2007 — Curtis, Vitaminwater, and G-Unit Books, including Derelict

    Wikipedia says his third album, Curtis, debuted at No. 2 on the Billboard 200 behind Kanye West’s Graduation. The same year, Coca-Cola bought Glacéau (Vitaminwater); Wikipedia, citing Forbes, reports that Jackson, a minority shareholder after a 2004 spokesperson deal that included Formula 50, earned about $100 million after taxes — a figure later described in some bankruptcy coverage as confidential and “likely up to” that level. On January 4, 2007, Wikipedia says, he launched the G-Unit Books imprint with MTV/Pocket Books. One of that year’s novels is Derelict, co-credited to Relentless Aaron and 50 Cent. Simon & Schuster dates the trade paperback to July 24, 2007 (ISBN 9781416549499, 256 pages); Amazon lists July 12, 2007. Those date and page-count conflicts are spelled out on the Derelict page.

    Wikipedia G-Unit Books Simon & Schuster

  7. 2009–2011 — Before I Self Destruct, The 50th Law, and later books

    His fourth studio album, Before I Self Destruct, is listed on the albums discography. The 50th Law, with Robert Greene, appears on the G-Unit Books bibliography as 2009. Wikipedia says the imprint’s last listed book is Playground (2011). A 2012 Avery fitness book, Formula 50, with Jeff O’Connell, sits outside that imprint. SMS Audio (2011) and Street King Energy (2011) are among the ventures Wikipedia and contemporary press date to this period. A later game, 50 Cent: Blood on the Sand (2009), is listed in the videography.

    Albums discography G-Unit Books Wikipedia

  8. 2014–2020 — Animal Ambition and Power

    Wikipedia says his fifth studio album, Animal Ambition, arrived in June 2014 and debuted at No. 4 on the Billboard 200. The same year, Power, created by Courtney A. Kemp, premiered on Starz (June 7, 2014) and ran through February 9, 2020, after six seasons. The main Wikipedia filmography lists him as Kanan Stark (recurring, then main) and as a producer on the universe; the article’s prose also credits him as executive producer through G-Unit Film and Television, a 2010 revival of G-Unit Films. A 2015 Chapter 11 bankruptcy filing is a documented part of the public record; court-docket figures that have been disputed in the press are not restated here.

    Wikipedia Albums discography

  9. 2020–present — Books, the Power universe, BMF, Super Bowl LVI, The Final Lap

    Hustle Harder, Hustle Smarter was published April 28, 2020, by Amistad / HarperCollins (304 pages, ISBN 978-0-06-295380-3), per Kirkus and bookseller listings. Wikipedia’s prose says he executive-produced Power spin-offs — Power Book II: Ghost (he also appears as Kanan in season 2, per the filmography table), Power Book III: Raising Kanan, and Power Book IV: Force — and Starz’s BMF (from September 2021), and performed the BMF theme “Wish Me Luck.” Early 2020 announcements briefly numbered Force differently; current official titles are used on those pages. He performed in the Super Bowl LVI halftime show on February 13, 2022. The Final Lap Tour in 2023 marked 20 years of Get Rich or Die Tryin’. Later acting credits on the merged filmography include Expend4bles (2023), Boneyard (2024), Skillhouse (2025), and a listed role as Balrog in a 2026 Street Fighter film (Hollywood Reporter, Sept. 4, 2025, cited on Wikipedia). A sixth studio album has not been listed as released on the albums discography; Street King Immortal remains an unreleased/delayed project.

    Wikipedia Kirkus Super Bowl LVI halftime show

  10. Gaps still open

    Exact episode titles and air dates for many talk-show appearances are only partly filled in on Wikipedia’s two filmography tables, which do not always agree.
