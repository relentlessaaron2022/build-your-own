Tips that follow the book

  1. Do the sounds for real. When the bucket goes BOOM, say it and tap a pot. When the bike bell goes RING, say it in a bright voice. When the girl claps, clap. Kids this age remember a story they got to help make.
  2. Give out parts. One person is the bucket, one is the bell, one is the clap. When the book hits the “too loud” page, let everyone crash together, then reset and take turns, just as Ricky does.
  3. Pause on Ricky's notebook. Ask, “What sound should he draw next?” It keeps a younger child looking, not only listening.
  4. Keep it to one sitting. This is a short comic. You do not need to stretch it across a week. The satisfaction of reaching the last page is part of the gift.
  5. Go straight to the activity page. “Make a Beat at Home” is the natural encore: a pot or bucket, a clap, a spoon on a cup, taking turns, and listening close. Details are on the activity page.

Bedtime, after school, or a rainy Sunday all work. If you are giving the book as a gift, you can tell the grown-up who will read it that the sound words are half the fun. For more on age and format, see the reading guide.
