This page does not claim the book is on a required list or aligned to a specific set of standards. It is a practical companion for a teacher, librarian, or homeschool parent who wants discussion questions and a simple activity that already live in the story.

Discussion questions

  1. What sounds did Ricky hear on his block? Which ones would you have noticed?
  2. Why did Ricky write the sounds in his notebook instead of only listening once?
  3. The girl on the stoop asks, “Can I be in your song?” What does Ricky do, and why does that matter?
  4. Why was the first try “too loud”? What changed when the group took turns?
  5. The older man says, “Everything has a beat, young man,” and later, “I just listened.” What do those lines mean in this story?
  6. What sounds would be in a “song of our classroom” or “song of our street”?
  7. How does Ricky act when the first try goes wrong? What can we copy from that?

A classroom beat, in five steps

The book's own closing page is ready for a rug or a music corner:

  1. Find a pot or bucket. BOOM!
  2. Clap your hands. CLAP!
  3. Tap a spoon on a cup. TINK!
  4. Take turns with your class.
  5. Listen close. Everything has a beat!

Assign parts, try it all at once so students can hear the crash, then try it in order, the way the comic does. The official activity art is on the Make a Beat at Home page. For values language you can use with families, see what kids learn.
