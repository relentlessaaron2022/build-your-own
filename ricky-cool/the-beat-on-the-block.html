The story, in parent language

Ricky steps out onto his block with his headphones and his notebook and says good morning to the street. Then he really listens. An older man in a flat cap is drumming on a bucket: BOOM, BOOM. A kid in a red helmet rings a bike bell: RING, RING. A girl with a purple headband claps on the stoop: CLAP, CLAP. Ricky writes each sound down. He is making a song from the block.

A bus sighing at the corner, a broom on the sidewalk, and a dog behind a fence all find their way into the notebook too. When Ricky finally has a whole band, he stands on the stoop like a bandleader and counts the neighbors in.

The first try is a happy mess — everyone plays at once, and the girl covers her ears and giggles, “Too loud!” Ricky taps his foot, shows them the order in his notebook, and they try again. Bucket first. Then bell. Then claps. This time the sounds fit together. Neighbors lean out of windows. A woman taps her watering can. A grandma shakes her keys. The whole block is playing.

At the end of the day, Ricky records the song, writes the title in his notebook, and leaves the reader with the line the book has been building toward: everything has a beat.

A soft ending you can keep going at home

The book finishes with a “Make a Beat at Home” page: find a pot or bucket, clap, tap a spoon on a cup, take turns, and listen close. It is a natural next step after you close the comic, and it is one of the reasons this book works as a parent-and-child purchase, not only as something to hand a child and walk away from.
