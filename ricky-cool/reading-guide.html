Ages 4–5: a sound-effect read-aloud

At this age, you will probably do most of the reading. That is not a drawback here. The comic is built on big, clear sound words — BOOM, RING, CLAP, PSSSH, SWISH, WOOF — and on pictures a child can follow even if they cannot read every balloon yet. Let your child shout the sounds, turn the pages, and point to the bucket, the bike, and the girl on the stoop. When the book is over, do the five-step activity at the back.

Ages 6–8: a first full-color comic

Many children in this range can handle a short comic with support, and some can read a lot of it themselves. The story is linear and short — the print edition is prepared as 34 pages — so a child can finish it in one sitting. The pictures carry the plot when a word is hard. If your child is not yet ready for a chapter book but is hungry for a “real book” that feels like theirs, a comic like this is a practical next step. See the reluctant-reader page if that is your situation.

About Ricky being 10

The lead is a 10-year-old, a little older than the top of the listed range. For a lot of families that is a plus: younger kids get a hero who already walks the block, writes in a notebook, and leads a group, without the story becoming a middle-grade novel. If you want a longer walkthrough of the plot before you buy, read what happens in Book 1.
