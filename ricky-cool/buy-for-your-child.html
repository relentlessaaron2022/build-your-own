The case for buying it

  1. It is actually short. This is not a six-hour middle-grade novel. It is a full-color comic — the print edition is prepared as 34 pages — so a child can reach the last page and feel finished.
  2. It gives you something to do together. The sounds are made to be read out loud, and the last page is a five-step family beat. You are not only handing over a file. You are buying a small evening.
  3. The values are in the plot. Listening, taking turns, and making room for a neighbor who asks to join are not stapled on. They are how the song gets written.
  4. The lead is easy to like. Ricky is a 10-year-old Black boy from Philadelphia with headphones and a notebook, an old soul who treats his block with respect.
  5. You can give it today, and paper is close. The Kindle edition is live. If you would rather wrap a physical copy, the paperback is coming next week — useful for a birthday, a classroom, or a Christmas 2026 stocking.
