Jazz promotion has to sound like the room, not like a blast

A lot of so-called music promotion is a stack of hashtags and a hope that the algorithm is kind. That is a weak fit for jazz. Your listeners may come from the local scene, from students of the music, from people who still read liner notes, or from younger players who found you through a live clip. Promotion has to speak to those people without talking down to them.

The work we do is practical: search-ready pages, social posts written in your voice, articles that can be found later, and a brand newsletter you can send to a list you already have or are starting. We deliver the work. We do not post to your accounts unless that is agreed in writing.

What a promotion cycle can actually cover

A honest jazz promotion cycle might cover a release, a run of dates, a new lineup, or a teaching project tied to the music. It should not invent a celebrity story you do not have. It should not promise that a post will fill a room. It should make it easier for the people already inclined to care to find the date, the record, and the name.

100 social posts across LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, Threads, and X give you language you can actually use. 50 articles give you longer pieces that search engines and people can find after the week is over. The web pages hold the story in one place.

After the first announcement

The quiet week after a release or a festival date is where most independent promotion dies. A newsletter, a set of pages that remain live, and articles that answer lasting questions — how the record was made, what the group sounds like, where to start in the catalog — are how the work keeps traveling.
