Your jazz catalog needs a home that is not only a streaming page

Jazz releases still matter as records: a debut leader date, a live set captured in a room, a standards session, a suite. Streaming pages are a storefront, not a story. They rarely explain why the record exists, who is on it, what the date felt like, or which track a new listener should start with.

Promoting your jazz music means writing around the release itself — the players, the room, the writing, the year — so a person who finds you through search or a shared link can hear the album the way you meant it to be heard.

A release week is not a marketing plan

Many independent jazz records get a flurry of posts on release day and then go quiet. The music does not expire on Friday. Listeners find records months later, often after a live date, a radio spin, or a recommendation from another player. Pages and articles that stay up are how a release keeps working after the first week.

The bundle gives that catalog a set of pages, a run of posts you can use at release and after, 50 articles that answer real questions about the music, and a newsletter written in your name. We do not promise playlist adds, radio spins, or chart numbers. We build a clear, lasting home for the work.

What the writing should carry

Good copy for a jazz release names the personnel, the original writing versus the standards, the recording context, and where to listen or buy. It does not drown the music in empty praise. It also does not hide the record behind a generic “new music out now” line that could belong to anyone.

If you have more than one title, the pages can distinguish them. If you have a single that is meant to open the door to a larger date, the writing can say that plainly.
