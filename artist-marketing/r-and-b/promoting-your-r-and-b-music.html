An R&B record needs more than a cover and a pre-save

Promoting your R&B music means treating the song as a piece of work: who wrote it, what it is about, how it sits next to the last release, and where a listener can go after the hook. Pre-save graphics are a moment. They are not a catalog.

Independent R&B catalogs often live as a trail of singles. That can be a sound strategy. It still needs pages that gather the story so a person who loved one track can find the next, the EP, or the visual world around it.

The mid-tempo record and the long listen

A lot of R&B is built for repeat listening — cars, headphones, late rooms — not only for a first-week spike. Writing that lasts, and pages that stay up, matter more here than a single burst of noise. Articles can answer the questions people actually type: what the song is about, where to start in your catalog, what this release is for.

We do not promise playlist placement or a viral moment. We write and design a home for the music, a run of posts you can use, and a newsletter that can carry a release or a personal note in your voice.

Features, visuals, and the next release

If a feature is part of the record, the writing should say so clearly and point back to your work, not only to the guest. If a visual is part of how the song should be seen, say that too. No. This is marketing and writing around the music you already have, not audio production.
