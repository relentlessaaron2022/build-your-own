Independent neo-soul artists need a public room, not a billboard

The music often comes from a band in a room, a writer at a keyboard, a singer who treats phrasing as seriously as a horn player. The public face should feel like a listening room, not like a clearance sale. That still means practical facts: the name, the city if it matters, the catalog, the next date or release.

Marketing for independent neo-soul artists is the work of writing that room into existence online — five pages with different angles, so the story is not one repeated paragraph.

The word “neo-soul” is a door, not a cage

Some artists wear the word easily. Others fear it has become a dusty box. We will use the language that fits the records, including soul, alternative R&B, or jazz-leaning descriptions when they are true. We will not stamp a trend label on music that does not ask for it.

100 social posts, 50 articles, and a newsletter follow the same care. If research finds a true sub-lane, up to three sub-genre newsletters may be included.
