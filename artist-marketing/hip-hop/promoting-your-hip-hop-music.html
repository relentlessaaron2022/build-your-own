Treat the drop as a record, not only as a moment

Promoting your hip-hop music means dating the release, naming the project, and giving people a way into the work after the first 24 hours. Mixtapes, EPs, street singles, and albums each need different language. A page that talks about all of them as “new heat” helps no one.

If the project has a point of view — a city, a story, a formal idea — the writing should carry it. If it is a lean batch of records meant to be played loud, the writing can say that too, without dressing it up as something it is not.

Catalog is a weapon if people can navigate it

Older records should not vanish when a new single lands. A simple, durable set of pages can point from the latest track back to the project that built your name, and from a featured verse back to your own catalog. That is unglamorous work. It is also how listeners become listeners of the artist, not of one clip.

50 articles in the bundle can take on lasting questions: how to start with your music, what a project is about, the story of a title, or the people on the record. We write from what you give us and what we can verify, not from filler.
