A hip-hop artist needs a name that can be found on purpose

City, cadence, and catalog still matter. So does a page that a program director, a collaborator, a journalist, or a new listener can actually read. Too many independent rappers are visible for a week and unsearchable the week after, because the only record of the work lives on a stale profile or a buried post.

Marketing for independent hip-hop artists, as we practice it, is the work of making the artist legible: who you are, where you are from if that is part of the story, what the music is, and what is out now. It is not a rewrite of your personality. It is not a promise that the culture will “pick you.”

Independent does not mean invisible

We will not invent a movement around you. We will not fake numbers. If the music and the brand are real, we can help people find them.
