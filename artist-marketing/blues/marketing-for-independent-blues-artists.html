A working blues artist still needs a page that works

Festival forms, club bookers, festival-goers, and record buyers all look you up. If they find a broken link, an old photo, and a bio that could belong to anyone with a guitar, the music has to do all the work alone. Sometimes it can. Often it should not have to.

Marketing for independent blues artists is the unglamorous work of making a true public face: who you are, what kind of blues you play, who is in the band, what is out, and how to reach the music.

Heritage language has to be earned

Blues marketing goes wrong when it leans on a borrowed myth. We will not write you into a history you do not have. We will write the history you do have — towns, teachers, bands, records — with care, and we will leave the rest alone.

Five web pages, 100 social posts, 50 articles, and a newsletter give that story a place to live. Sub-genre newsletters, up to three, are added only if research finds a real sub-lane.
