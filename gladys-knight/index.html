A career in progress

This timeline is a work in progress. Every entry links to a public source. Where a date or detail couldn't be verified, it's marked as a gap and left unfilled.

Gaps: the exact date of the Original Amateur Hour win; the dates of her 1980s–1990s TV guest spots; full 2026 tour dates; and a complete list of anthem performances.

  1. 1944 — Born in Atlanta

    Gladys Maria Knight was born May 28, 1944, in Atlanta, Georgia.

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight

  2. c. 1952 — Original Amateur Hour

    At about age eight she won Ted Mack's The Original Amateur Hour singing Nat King Cole's “Too Young.” Exact air date: gap.

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight

  3. 1952–1957 — The Pips form

    A family group with her brother Merald “Bubba” Knight, sister Brenda, and cousins forms after Bubba's 10th birthday party, named for cousin James “Pip” Woods. Signed to Brunswick in 1957.

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight

  4. 1961 — “Every Beat of My Heart”

    First hit: No. 6 pop, No. 1 R&B.

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight & the Pips discography

  5. 1966 — Motown

    Gladys Knight & the Pips join Motown, recording for its Soul label.

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight

  6. 1967 — “I Heard It Through the Grapevine”

    No. 1 R&B for six weeks and No. 2 on the Billboard Hot 100; Motown's best-selling single to that point.

    Billboard: Gladys Knight & the Pips chart history Wikipedia: I Heard It Through the Grapevine

  7. 1973 — Buddah and “Midnight Train to Georgia”

    The group moves to Buddah Records. “Midnight Train to Georgia” goes No. 1 pop and R&B.

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight Billboard: Gladys Knight & the Pips chart history

  8. 1974 — First two Grammys

    Wins for “Midnight Train to Georgia” and “Neither One of Us.” The Claudine soundtrack with Curtis Mayfield follows.

    GRAMMY.com: Gladys Knight GRAMMY.com: GRAMMY Rewind, 1974

  9. 1976 — Acting debut

    Lead role in Pipe Dreams; Golden Globe nomination for New Star of the Year.

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight IMDb: Pipe Dreams (1976)

  10. 1978–1980 — Solo LPs, then Columbia

    Legal issues force separate recording: Miss Gladys Knight (1978) and Gladys Knight (1979). The group reunites on Columbia in 1980.

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight

  11. 1983 — “Save the Overtime (For Me)”

    No. 1 R&B, the group's first R&B chart-topper since 1974.

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight Wikipedia: Gladys Knight & the Pips discography

  12. 1985–1986 — “That's What Friends Are For”

    With Dionne Warwick, Elton John and Stevie Wonder: No. 1 Hot 100 for four weeks; Grammy win.

    Billboard: Gladys Knight & the Pips chart history GRAMMY.com: Gladys Knight

  13. 1987–1988 — “Love Overboard” and the Pips' last bow

    Final group LP All Our Love; “Love Overboard” wins a Grammy. The Pips retire after a 1988 tour.

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight GRAMMY.com: Gladys Knight

  14. 1989 — Licence to Kill

    Sings the James Bond title song; Top 10 in the UK.

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight Wikipedia: Gladys Knight discography

  15. 1991 — Good Woman

    No. 1 R&B album. Also sings the anthem at Game 1 of the World Series.

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight Wikipedia: Gladys Knight discography

  16. 1995 — Hollywood Walk of Fame

    Receives a star.

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight

  17. 1996 — Rock and Roll Hall of Fame

    Inducted with the Pips.

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight Rock & Roll Hall of Fame: Gladys Knight & the Pips

  18. 2001–2005 — Solo and gospel Grammys

    At Last wins Best Traditional R&B Vocal Album; “Heaven Help Us All” with Ray Charles and One Voice with Saints Unified Voices win in gospel categories.

    GRAMMY.com: Gladys Knight

  19. 2007 — “Empress of Soul”

    Society of Singers Ella Award, where she is declared the “Empress of Soul.”

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight

  20. 2012 — Dancing with the Stars

    Season 14 contestant.

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight GRAMMY.com: 2024 Lifetime Achievement honorees

  21. 2019 — Super Bowl LIII and The Masked Singer

    Sings the anthem in Atlanta; places third as “Bee.”

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight IMDb: Super Bowl LIII GRAMMY.com: 2024 Lifetime Achievement honorees

  22. 2021–2022 — National honors

    National Medal of Arts (2021 class), then the 45th Kennedy Center Honors in 2022.

    Wikipedia: Gladys Knight GRAMMY.com: 2024 Lifetime Achievement honorees

  23. 2024 — Recording Academy Lifetime Achievement Award

    Named a 2024 honoree. (Wikipedia's body text says 2023; see the conflicts note.)

    GRAMMY.com: 2024 Lifetime Achievement honorees Wikipedia: Gladys Knight

  24. Aug.–Nov. 2026 — Fewer stops

    She announces she is “easing into fewer performances,” later says the “midnight train” will make “just a few more stops,” and plays her last scheduled 2026 date on Nov. 14 in Highland, Calif. She has not formally announced retirement.

    CBS News Atlanta, Aug. 28, 2026 WSB-TV, Sept. 14, 2026 That Grape Juice, Nov. 17, 2026
