Child safety tips for parents

Safety habits are not a guarantee, and this page will not pretend they are. They are still worth practicing: know how to reach your child, know who is allowed to pick them up, and use official guides for online safety instead of viral lists with no source.

Use official guides, not a random list invented here

NCMEC keeps education material at missingkids.org/education and online-safety material at NetSmartz (the /netsmartz path redirects to /netsmartz/home; both were checked). Those pages are the living source. This draft will not invent a “top ten rules” chart as if it were a new official standard.

Plain habits worth repeating

  • Keep current photos and basic identifying information where you can reach them quickly if you ever need to make a report.
  • Make sure your child knows your real phone number and a meeting plan if you get separated in a public place.
  • Know who is allowed to pick your child up from school or practice, and tell the school.
  • Talk about online approaches in age-appropriate language, using official guides, not scare videos.

If the worst happens

Do not wait. Call 911 and follow NCMEC’s Get Help Now guidance. Time matters, and official process matters more than a social post.

Questions

Does following these tips mean a child cannot go missing?

No. They are habits, not a promise.

Should I put a tracking device on my child?

That is a family decision, and not something this draft will mandate. Official sites are a better place to weigh tools than a micro page.
