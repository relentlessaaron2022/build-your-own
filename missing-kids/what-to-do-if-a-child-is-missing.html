Awareness and resources

What to do if a child is missing

If a child is missing, this page is not the plan. The plan is: call 911, then use NCMEC’s official guidance and 1-800-THE-LOST (1-800-843-5678). Every minute spent hunting for advice on a random site is a minute better spent on official help.

Do this first

  1. Call 911. Local police need to be notified right away.
  2. Call NCMEC at 1-800-THE-LOST (1-800-843-5678) and use Get Help Now.
  3. Give officers a current photo and accurate descriptions. Do not guess facts you do not know.

NCMEC’s own page is the checklist to follow, because they update it. This draft will not invent a competing checklist.

AMBER Alerts and official tips

Official alert programs, including AMBER Alert (amberalert.ojp.gov, HTTP 200), are run through law enforcement, not through a fan site. If you see an official alert, follow what it says. If you have a tip, give it to police, NCMEC, or tips.fbi.gov.

What not to do

Do not wait a set number of hours because of a myth you heard. Do not confront someone you “think” is involved. Do not post a child’s location details that police have asked you to hold back.

Questions

Is there a waiting period to report a missing child?

Do not wait. Call 911. Follow official guidance, not a rumor about a 24-hour rule.

Should I go search on my own?

Tell the police what you know and follow their instructions. A freelance search can interfere with an official one.
