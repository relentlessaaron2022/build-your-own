What is an RCFE in California?

RCFE stands for Residential Care Facility for the Elderly. It's the state license behind most board and care homes and assisted living communities in California.

The short version

An RCFE provides housing, meals, care and supervision for people generally 60 and older who need help with daily living but don't need 24-hour skilled nursing. It is licensed by the California Department of Social Services (CDSS), Community Care Licensing Division, under the California Residential Care Facilities for the Elderly Act and the regulations in Title 22 of the California Code of Regulations.

What an RCFE provides

  • A room (private or shared), meals and housekeeping.
  • Help with activities of daily living: bathing, dressing, grooming, toileting, moving around.
  • Help with medications.
  • Supervision and someone on duty around the clock.
  • Activities and social contact.

What an RCFE is not

  • Not a nursing home. Skilled nursing facilities are licensed by the California Department of Public Health and provide 24-hour nursing care. RCFEs are non-medical, though many work with home health and hospice agencies.
  • Not a health plan. "Managed care" in California usually means a Medi-Cal or Medicare health plan. An RCFE is a place to live, not an insurer.
  • Not home care. In-home caregivers come to your parent's house. An RCFE is the house.

Board and care vs assisted living

Both terms usually describe RCFEs. "Board and care" usually means a small home with six or fewer residents. "Assisted living" more often means a larger community. Same license, different scale. Compare them.

How to look up any home

  1. Search the home's name or address on the CDSS care facility search.
  2. Check the license number, status, capacity, and the inspection and complaint reports.
  3. In San Diego County, Choose Well San Diego also publishes profiles that combine state data with details from the home.
