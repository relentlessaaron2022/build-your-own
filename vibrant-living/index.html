Both homes are licensed by the California Department of Social Services as Residential Care Facilities for the Elderly (RCFEs), the license type most people call "board and care." Both are operated by Greater Solutions, with Julie Norris listed as administrator on the state record. The first Vibrant Living home was licensed in San Diego in 2014, so this is a family of homes with more than a decade in the work.

Who a 6-bed home is for

A board and care home suits an older adult who can't safely live alone anymore but doesn't need a hospital or skilled nursing facility: someone who needs help with bathing, dressing, medications, meals or getting around, plus people around them day and night. In a home with six residents, caregivers know each person's routine, the meals come out of a home kitchen, and the setting looks like the house your parent lived in, not a campus. How a 6-bed home compares with a large community.

What the public records say

  • Hospice: state license notes list a hospice waiver at both homes, so a resident can stay in the home and receive care from a licensed hospice agency. What that means.
  • Non-ambulatory residents: both homes' fire clearances allow up to six non-ambulatory residents. What "non-ambulatory" covers.
  • Medi-Cal Assisted Living Waiver: the Lance Place home is listed as accepting the waiver. How the ALW works.
  • Languages: English and Spanish, per the Lance Place Choose Well profile.

Daily life

In the homes' own published description, the focus is helping each resident reach the highest level of functioning they can, through social stimulation, attention to diet, and a supportive environment, with weekly activities including fitness and monthly events.
