Medi-Cal ALW

Joining and operating under the Assisted Living Waiver

The Medi-Cal Assisted Living Waiver lets RCFEs in participating counties, including San Diego, serve residents whose care is paid by Medi-Cal.

Not legal advice. This is a plain-language overview for operators. Requirements change, so confirm everything with the CDSS Community Care Licensing Division, the current Title 22 regulations, and your own advisors.

How the money works

The waiver pays providers for assisted living services, at a rate tied to the resident's assessed level of care. Room and board is paid separately by the resident, at an amount set under program rules. Model your budget on both, because a waiver resident's room-and-board payment is typically lower than private-pay rent.

Becoming a provider

  • A licensed RCFE in good standing, in a participating county.
  • Enrollment as a provider for the waiver.
  • Meeting the federal home- and community-based settings requirements for residents' rights and community access.
  • Working relationships with the Care Coordination Agencies (CCAs) that serve your county.

Check the current DHCS Assisted Living Waiver pages and provider bulletins for enrollment steps. This page is only an outline.

Working with Care Coordination Agencies

CCAs assess applicants, help place them, write the service plan and monitor care. Responsive, reliable homes get calls, so keep CCAs informed about openings and changes in residents' condition.

What families need to hear from you

Being on a waiver provider list isn't the same as having an open waiver bed. Be clear about availability. Our family-side explanation: the ALW in San Diego.
