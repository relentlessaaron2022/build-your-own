Multi-site

Running two small homes, not one

A second six-bed house can be a better way to grow than adding beds, but only if both homes run to the same standard.

Not legal advice. This is a plain-language overview for operators. Requirements change, so confirm everything with the CDSS Community Care Licensing Division, the current Title 22 regulations, and your own advisors.

Each house is its own license

Every home has its own license number, fire clearance, waivers, inspections and record, and families see each one separately on the state site. Keep a one-page "license sheet" per home: number, capacity, waivers, last inspection and open items.

Consistent standards

  • One set of policies and forms used in both homes.
  • The same medication system and record format.
  • Cross-training so caregivers can cover either house without guessing.
  • Regular administrator presence in both, on a schedule staff can predict.

Scheduling across sites

Floaters who know both homes are valuable. But residents do best with the same faces, so keep core staff anchored to one house and use floaters for gaps.

Keeping listings straight

Two homes with similar names confuse directories. It's common to see one house's address on the other's listing, an old address that has closed, or a ZIP code that doesn't match the license. Families who land on the wrong listing call the wrong number or drive to the wrong house. Audit every listing for both homes at least twice a year. More on listings.
