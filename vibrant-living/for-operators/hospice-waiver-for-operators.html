Hospice waiver

Hospice waivers and working with hospice agencies

A hospice waiver lets residents stay in your home at the end of life. For families, that's often the deciding factor.

Call (619) 971-8431Schedule a tourDirections
Not legal advice. This is a plain-language overview for operators. Requirements change, so confirm everything with the CDSS Community Care Licensing Division, the current Title 22 regulations, and your own advisors.

Why get one

Without a hospice waiver, a resident who enters hospice may have to move at the hardest time in their family's life. With one, the resident can stay, cared for by your staff and a licensed hospice agency together. Families often ask about hospice on the first call.

What it involves

  • A request to Community Care Licensing and approval for a stated number of hospice residents.
  • A written hospice care plan for each hospice resident, coordinated with the agency.
  • Staff who are trained for the needs involved and know how to reach hospice around the clock.
  • Fire clearance and staffing that fit residents who may become bedridden.

Working with agencies

  • Know who the hospice nurse is for each resident, and how to reach on-call staff at night.
  • Agree on who documents what, and keep the hospice plan in the resident's file.
  • Include the family in care conferences.
  • Respect the resident's choice of hospice agency.

Explaining it to families

Tell families clearly how many hospice residents your waiver allows and how many you have now. Our family-side explanation: hospice in a board and care home.
