Staffing

Staffing and caregiver training in a 6-bed RCFE

In a six-bed home, every caregiver matters. One great hire lifts the whole house. One bad one is felt by every resident.

Call (619) 971-8431Schedule a tourDirections
Not legal advice. This is a plain-language overview for operators. Requirements change, so confirm everything with the CDSS Community Care Licensing Division, the current Title 22 regulations, and your own advisors.

Coverage

Title 22 requires enough staff on duty to meet residents' needs at all times, with someone on the premises around the clock. What "enough" means depends on who lives in the home. A house with non-ambulatory residents, two-person transfers, or residents with dementia who are up at night needs more coverage than a house of mostly independent residents. Ask yourself: if the hardest night happens, can the person on shift handle it safely?

Required training

California sets minimum training for direct-care staff in RCFEs: initial training, part of it before working independently with residents, plus annual ongoing training, with dementia-care content included. Homes with hospice waivers or dementia programs have additional expectations. Check the current CDSS requirements and keep certificates in each personnel file.

Before the first shift

  • Criminal record clearance or an approved transfer.
  • Required health screening, including tuberculosis testing.
  • Orientation to the house, residents' care plans, emergency procedures and residents' rights.

Keeping good caregivers

  • Predictable schedules and fair pay for overnight shifts.
  • Training that leads somewhere, such as medication or dementia specialization.
  • A clear person to call, every time, when something goes wrong.
  • Recognition. Families notice good caregivers, so tell your staff when they do.
