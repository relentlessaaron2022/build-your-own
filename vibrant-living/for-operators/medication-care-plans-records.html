Records

Medication management, care plans and records that survive an inspection

Many citations in small homes aren't about bad care. They're about care that wasn't written down correctly.

Not legal advice. This is a plain-language overview for operators. Requirements change, so confirm everything with the CDSS Community Care Licensing Division, the current Title 22 regulations, and your own advisors.

Medications

  • Keep centrally stored medications locked, labeled, and in their original pharmacy containers.
  • Keep a current medication list for every resident that matches the physician's orders.
  • Record each dose assisted, at the time, by the person who assisted.
  • For as-needed (PRN) medications, follow the written instructions and record when and why each dose was given and how it worked.
  • Have a clear process for refusals, errors, discontinued medications and disposal.
  • Train every caregiver who assists with medications, and keep proof in their file.

Appraisals and care plans

Before admission, appraise the resident's needs and confirm your home can meet them. Reappraise when their condition changes, not just on a calendar. A care plan should say what help the resident needs, how often, who provides it, and what to watch for. It should read like instructions a new caregiver could follow.

The resident file

Keep each resident's admission agreement, physician's report, appraisals, care plan, medication records, incident reports and notes in one file, in the same order for every resident. Consistency is what lets you, and an analyst, find things.

Monthly self-audit

  1. Pick two resident files at random and check them against a list.
  2. Compare a few medications on hand against the record.
  3. Check that every incident in the month was reported on time.
  4. Fix what you find, and write down that you did.
