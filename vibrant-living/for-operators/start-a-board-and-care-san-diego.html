Start-up

Starting a 6-bed board and care in San Diego

The practical side of opening a small RCFE in San Diego, from the house to the first resident.

Call (619) 971-8431Schedule a tourDirections
Not legal advice. This is a plain-language overview for operators. Requirements change, so confirm everything with the CDSS Community Care Licensing Division, the current Title 22 regulations, and your own advisors.

The house

  • Single-story is easier. Stairs limit who you can accept and complicate fire clearance.
  • Bedrooms and bathrooms. Work out how many residents each room can hold under the rules, and how many bathrooms you'll need.
  • Hallways, doorways and showers wide enough for wheelchairs and walkers, if you plan to accept non-ambulatory residents.
  • Outdoor space residents can use safely.

Zoning

Under California law, an RCFE serving six or fewer residents is treated as a residential use of property for zoning purposes. Confirm the details with the City of San Diego, and check your lease, HOA rules and insurance policy too.

Fire clearance

Your fire clearance sets how many non-ambulatory or bedridden residents you can serve. Alarms, sprinklers, exits and other features may be required depending on who you serve. Talk to the fire authority early. Changing a house later is expensive.

Capital and cash flow

Plan for the house (lease or purchase), furnishings and equipment, insurance, licensing and certification costs, and payroll before you reach full occupancy. Many homes take months to fill, and the ones that survive budget for that.

A realistic sequence

  1. Orientation and administrator certification.
  2. House selection and fire-authority conversations.
  3. Application packet and background checks.
  4. Pre-licensing visit and license.
  5. Marketing, tours and first admissions.

The licensing steps are covered in how to get an RCFE license.
