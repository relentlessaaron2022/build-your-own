End-of-life care

Hospice in a board and care home

Many families want a parent to spend their last months somewhere familiar, not in a hospital. In California, a board and care home needs a hospice waiver to make that possible.

What a hospice waiver is

RCFEs are non-medical settings. To let residents who are on hospice stay and receive care in the home, the licensee must have approval from Community Care Licensing, commonly called a hospice waiver. With that approval, a resident can choose a licensed hospice agency, and the agency's nurses, aides, social workers and chaplains visit the home while the home's caregivers provide day-to-day care.

Vibrant Living's waivers, per public records

  • Lance Place: state license notes list a hospice waiver for up to three residents; the Choose Well profile describes two hospice waivers. Ask the home for the current number.
  • Crawford Street: state license notes list a hospice waiver for up to six residents.

Questions to ask

  1. How many hospice residents can the home have right now?
  2. Which hospice agencies have you worked with?
  3. How do your caregivers reach hospice after hours?
  4. Can family stay late near the end?

If hospice may be needed soon, say so when you call so the home can tell you what's available. Contact Vibrant Living.
