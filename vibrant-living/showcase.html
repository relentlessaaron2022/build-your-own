Why it was built this way

Families do not only search for “assisted living in San Diego.” They search for a small home, a neighborhood, hospice, a Medi-Cal waiver, or how to read a state inspection. Large campuses already dominate those generic searches. A 6-bed home is found when each house, and each real question, has its own clear page.

That is why Lance Place and Crawford Street each have a dedicated page, with the correct license number, ZIP code, and neighborhood. Some directories still mis-file Crawford Street as 92120 or Grantville. These pages use the state record: 92123, Serra Mesa.

The operator guides exist because people who want to run a small home like yours also search, and because a working two-home example is more useful than a generic manual. Those pages teach at a high level and are not legal advice. They do not publish your staffing roster or your rates.

Click-to-call is on every family page because the next step is a tour, not an online application. GitHub Pages cannot host checkout, and admissions should stay on the phone and email you already use.

Only facts from the California CDSS / CHHS licensing record, your Choose Well San Diego profile, and the pages already built from that public record were used for claims about the homes.

Facts left off, pending your confirmation

These items showed up in directories or placement sites, but not clearly enough to publish as current fact about your homes. Nothing below has been added as a hard claim. If you want any of it public, say the word and it goes on. If you want it kept off, it stays off.

  1. Current monthly rates. Public directories do not agree with each other (Choose Well lists one range; Caring.com shows another, including a stale listing for a closed address). The cost page explains how to read a quote. It does not publish a Vibrant Living rate.
  2. Staffing counts. One directory lists day and night numbers. Those figures are not published as a current fact about your homes.
  3. A memory-care page. Held until you confirm what you want said about memory care in these houses.
  4. A short-term or respite-stay page. A placement site says temporary stays are welcome. That is held until you confirm you offer them.
  5. Assisted Living Waiver openings. Lance Place appears on a public ALW list. A listing is not an open bed. Whether Crawford Street participates, and whether any waiver bed is open, needs your confirmation.
  6. Tour hours. Not published, pending the hours you want listed.
  7. Star ratings and review counts. Public sites do not agree, and some listings may be split or stale. No rating is on the pages.
