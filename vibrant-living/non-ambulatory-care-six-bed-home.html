Whether a small home can accept someone who uses a wheelchair or can't get up alone often comes down to one line in the license: its fire clearance.

What "non-ambulatory" means

In California care licensing, a non-ambulatory person is someone who can't leave the building unassisted in an emergency. That includes people who depend on a wheelchair, walker or another person to get out, and people who can't respond to an alarm on their own. It is a fire-safety definition, not a diagnosis.

Why fire clearance matters

Every RCFE has a fire clearance that limits how many residents can be non-ambulatory, and sometimes bedridden. A home can't accept more non-ambulatory residents than that number, however capable its staff. When you call a home, ask how many non-ambulatory residents it is cleared for and how many it has now.

Vibrant Living's clearances, per state records

  • Lance Place: approved fire clearance for six non-ambulatory residents.
  • Crawford Street: six residents, of whom six may be non-ambulatory.

What else to verify in person

  • Doorway widths, bathroom layout, and whether showers are roll-in.
  • Whether the home uses mechanical lifts, and whether caregivers are trained on them.
  • How many caregivers are on duty at night for two-person transfers.
  • How the home handles a resident who becomes bedbound, including the hospice waiver.

Bring your loved one's mobility details, equipment list and any recent hospital discharge notes to the tour. Schedule one.
