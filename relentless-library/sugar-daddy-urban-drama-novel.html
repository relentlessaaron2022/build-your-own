Sugar Daddy by Relentless Aaron: an urban drama

Sugar Daddy is an urban drama by Relentless Aaron. The Relentless Content edition calls it that on the title page. It follows Toy, a bartender, and Colin, who looks like a better bet until his work for a counterfeiting operator, Vince Reed, puts both of them in danger.

The premise, in plain language

Toy grew up in a world of fast money and learned, too early, what it meant to be treated as someone else’s prize. As a bartender she is used to being approached. Colin seems different: he has goals, he treats her with respect, he looks like a way out. Then the money behind him comes into view. He works for Vince Reed, a counterfeiting kingpin who runs people as if they were pieces on a board. Catalog copy says it is only a matter of time before Toy and Colin are used in a crueler game, with their lives at stake. That is the story as public listings tell it. This page stays non-explicit and does not invent later twists.

How the author labeled the book

The Relentless Content excerpt on file carries the subtitle “An Urban Drama,” a 2005 copyright line, and a short catalog-style summary: the “sugar daddy” figure is the source of money, comfort, and security when a woman is stranded — a man who talks like a guardian or companion until the cost of that comfort shows. Editors listed on that edition are Relentless Aaron and Rene Chester.
