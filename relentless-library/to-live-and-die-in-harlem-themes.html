Themes in the novel To Live and Die in Harlem

The sequel novel is about whether a man can claim a home he is trying to rebuild, and whether official places can be trusted when a criminal enterprise may already be inside them.

Find the novel

PlaceHarlem, including West 128th Street

WorkBrownstone renovation; company Melrah

PressureA missing reporter; alleged official corruption

ToneCrime suspense

Home as work, not a speech

Bookstore listings say there is one thing left worth fighting for: home. The printed book makes that concrete. Push is rewiring, insulating, and filling units. Congressman Percy Chambers’ Harlem Empowerment Fund is mentioned as a guarantee, not a cash gift. The theme is sweat equity and whether that work can hold when violence and politics close in.

A case that may reach official desks

Verified notes show Judge Charles Pullman telling Evelyn Watson that a Times reporter, Michelle Scott, had access to players in a group called L.T.K., and that he fears people inside local, state, and federal agencies. Those are a character’s claims in the book, not facts this page asserts about the real world. The theme is distrust of institutions, written as fiction.

Questions

Does the book explain L.T.K. in the opening?

In the photographed early chapters, Evelyn has to ask what L.T.K. is. This page does not invent an answer the notes did not contain.

Is this nonfiction about Harlem housing?

No. It is a novel.
