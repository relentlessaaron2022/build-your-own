Come on Thru: A Memory in Every Beat

I walk down the same street where my brother and I used to drop it. Each beat a memory, every verse a step closer home.

By Relentless AaronNovember 2, 2026Read the original at relentlessaaron.net

A Memory on Every Beat

I walk down the same street where my brother and I used to drop it. The concrete still holds the heat of those afternoons, but now it’s cooler, the kind that whispers through open windows late at night.

The Streets Were Our Canvas

We’d ride our bikes, coasting past empty lots and abandoned buildings. The corner store was like a second home, where we’d trade stories and dream big. On a warm summer afternoon, my brother and I spotted him under an old oak tree. He had a cassette player, his head tilted back as he listened to music.

I remember the song, “Come on Thru” by Relentless Aaron. The intro hits, and the bass drops heavy like a weight, but it’s familiar, comforting. It’s the sound that lured us closer, pulling us into the memories that define our days.

The Beat of Our Youth

The first verse kicks in, and I’m transported back to those stolen moments between classes. My brother’s lyrics paint a picture so vivid: “I used to ride my bike / Where the sun was bright and free.” I could almost feel the wind in my hair as I pedaled, the world moving slow.

Each line is like a flash from the past, “And we’d chase the sound of freedom / Until our laughter became our own.” Those moments were pure and raw, untainted by anything but the joy of youth. Now they’re captured in this song, immortalized on every beat.

Home Again

The outro kicks in, the song fades into a whisper, just like those afternoons when we’d park ourselves under that tree. The melody pulls at my heartstrings, and I can almost taste the lemonade from the store. This isn’t just music; it’s a journey back to where we started.

“Come on Thru” is more than a track, it’s a connection to the past. It reminds me of the days when the streets were our canvas, the sun was always shining, and everything felt possible.

The Cost of Memory

Every stream buys back a piece of what it cost. You’ve been paying the cost of not knowing long enough.
