Rise of The Titans, My First Show

Aaron’s first real gig in the heart of Atlanta, where the crowd felt the rawest and most genuine., #relentlessaaron #soul #hiphop

By Relentless AaronNovember 11, 2026Read the original at relentlessaaron.net

The Night It All Changed

It was January 2010, and I was 18 years old. I had been working on my music for about a year, pouring my heart and soul into lyrics that seemed too raw for the world to handle. My mom dropped me off at the club, and I was a bundle of nerves, clutching the CD cases of my demo tapes. The venue was the infamous Shelter in downtown Atlanta, a place where the street vibe was thick and the energy was electric.

Walking Through the Door

The Shelter had this old-school charm, with its dim lights and worn-out vinyl flooring. The crowd was a mix of regulars and newcomers, all here for the raw talent that was emerging. As I stepped inside, I felt a mix of excitement and dread. The air was charged with the promise of something big, and I knew this night could change everything.

The Soundcheck

Before the show, I had a soundcheck. The sound guy, a grizzled veteran with a thick Atlanta accent, hooked me up with a mic and a guitar. “Just play it like you’re talking to one person,” he said. “That’s the best way to reach them.” I nodded, feeling a mix of gratitude and terror. I plugged in my guitar and ran through a few lines, making sure the mic was just right. I could feel the sweat beading on my forehead, but I pushed through, knowing this was my moment.

“Rise of The Titans”, The Moment

The first song was “Rise of The Titans,” and it was the highlight of the night. The crowd went wild as the beat dropped, and I saw their faces shift from skepticism to genuine interest. The song was about my journey, my rise from the streets, and the cost of that journey. I rapped about the nights I stayed up late, the hard work, and the sacrifices. I hit the mic with all the emotion I could muster, pouring my heart into every word.

[Listen to “Rise of The Titans”: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RW7fxMiyuis]

The crowd responded with a mix of nods and cheers. I could feel the tension in the air, the anticipation for what was to come. As I finished the song, the crowd roared, and I felt a surge of confidence. I knew this was the start of something big.

The Aftermath

The night was a blur of high fives, hugs, and more offers to join different crews. I knew I had to stay true to myself, but the pressure was there. I walked out of the Shelter, my head spinning with all the possibilities. I knew that every stream of the song bought back a piece of what it cost. I was paying the price of not knowing, and now I was here, standing on the edge of something incredible.

Realizing the Cost

The next few months were a whirlwind. I worked tirelessly, honing my craft, and the song became my calling card. But the cost was high. I missed more nights with friends, more sleep, more personal time. I knew the real cost of not knowing long enough. I was paying the price, but I was also rising.
