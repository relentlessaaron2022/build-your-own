How to Make $100,000 in 90 Days: A Live Blueprint from the Fortress

I wrote books from behind bars and sold them hand-to-hand through the prison mail system.

By Relentless AaronApril 11, 2026Read the original at relentlessaaron.net

I wrote books from behind bars and sold them hand-to-hand through the prison mail system.

If I could engineer cash flow with a 6 a.m. count and a CO breathing down my neck — I can map out how to make $100,000 in 90 days from the Fortress in Atlanta.

That’s not a boast. That’s a baseline.

This is a live business document. The plan, the pressure, and the proof — written in real time. If you want motivation content, keep scrolling. If you want to watch a man who built an empire from a prison cell work a digital catalog and a content strategy until they produce six figures — you’re in the right place.

Let’s get into it.

Why I’m Putting My Business in the Street

Accountability is the cheat code most people are too scared to use.

Saying the number out loud — $100,000 in 90 days — changes your relationship with it. It stops being a fantasy. It becomes a deadline. That shift is everything.

Behavioral research on public commitment devices is clear: the same 90-day sprint, executed with public tracking, produces 3x more revenue than the identical plan run in private. Not because the plan changes. Because the stakes are real when people are watching.

I’m posting this because I need the weight of public commitment on my shoulders. That weight is a tool.

This isn’t inspiration content. If I miss the number, you’ll see that too. Wins get posted. Losses get posted. Pivots get documented. That’s the deal.

I wrote PUSH because it was the truth nobody else would tell — raw, unfiltered, straight from the gut of what street life actually looks like. This post runs the same energy. Applied to money, business, and the infrastructure of a legacy.

PUSH didn’t reach millions because it was safe. It reached millions because it was honest.

This 90-day plan is honest.

The Honest Starting Point

No origin story. Just the actual baseline.

The foundation is the catalog. Twenty-five-plus titles — THE LAST KINGPIN, FREEZE, TRIPLE THREAT, BUMRUSH, PUSH, PLATINUM DOLLS, TOPLESS, SUGAR DADDY, FIRE & DESIRE, EXTRA MARITAL AFFAIRS, LADY FIRST, SINGLE WITH BENEFITS, RAPPERS R IN DANGER, and more — all live at beacons.ai/gorelentless and ready to convert. This IP has millions of readers in its DNA. The audience was built before the algorithm existed.

The gap? Digital monetization is still being built.

These books moved through street channels, word of mouth, and hand-to-hand distribution. That’s a real foundation. But foundation and activation are two different things. Right now the catalog is a building with the lights off — solid structure, but not working 24/7 the way it needs to.

Active revenue lanes: direct sales through Beacons, sync licensing plays from the music catalog, Spotify streaming, and new content through relentlessaaron.net. Some lanes are producing. Some are trickling. Some are wide open and waiting for consistent traffic.

Street wisdom truth: you can’t fix what you won’t face.

Here’s the face of it. The potential in this catalog dwarfs the current return. That gap between what this IP should generate and what it actually generates — that’s the business opportunity. Closing it in 90 days is the mission.

The Federal Reserve’s 2023 Small Business Credit Survey confirms Black entrepreneurs are denied small business loans at twice the rate of white entrepreneurs. That’s not a complaint. That’s context. A digital-first, low-overhead, catalog-driven model isn’t a preference for creators who look like me. It’s a structural necessity. The Beacons store isn’t a side play. It’s the bank.

Starting from reality. Reality is workable.

The 90-Day Breakdown — Move by Move

The game is always about knowing which move generates which return. The kingpin doesn’t guess. He calculates.

Here’s the calculation.

Month 1 (Days 1–30): Lock the Foundation

Hard target: $20,000

First 30 days aren’t about scale. They’re about structure.

Full catalog optimization on the Beacons store — every title formatted, every description sharp, every price point tested. The conversion funnel gets built. Email list activation starts now. Every reader who’s ever touched a Relentless Aaron title is a potential repeat buyer, and they need a reason to come back.

The content engine on relentlessaaron.net launches at full force. Daily posts. Story-driven content. Real documentation of this journey. Every piece of content is a deliberate conversion opportunity — not a soft one.

$100,000 in 90 days breaks down to $1,111 per day. That sounds like pressure until you flip the math: two mid-ticket offers at $555 each, closed daily. Or one $3,333 deal every three days. Not impossible. Requires focus and consistency — exactly what Month 1 is about building.

Month 2 (Days 31–60): Amplify Everything

Hard target: $45,000 cumulative

Month 2 is where the engine gets fuel.

Paid traffic testing starts — small, targeted, aimed at audiences that already read urban fiction, street literature, and Black cultural content. The email list built in Month 1 gets activated with offers, story sequences, and bundle drops.

FREEZE and SUGAR DADDY get pushed into new audience segments — readers who haven’t found them yet but would with the right context. Both titles have crossover potential that’s never been fully tapped.

Sync licensing pitches go out. Music placement in film, TV, and digital media is a real check — and the music catalog sitting alongside the literary catalog is a weapon waiting to be used. Most authors never touch this revenue stream. I’m touching it.

Affiliate and partner plays get activated. Black creators, urban fiction platforms, cultural media that already carries the audience I’m building toward — these are alignment opportunities, not sales pitches.

Kevin Kelly’s 1,000 True Fans framework makes the math clear: 1,000 true fans at $100 average transaction value per quarter hits the $100K threshold. This catalog has more than 1,000 true fans in its history. The activation is the work.

Month 3 (Days 61–90): The Conversion Sprint

Hard target: $100,000 cumulative

Month 3 is war.

Bundle offers. Limited drops. Live community events — virtual and in-person where possible. Aggressive daily content. Every lane firing at the same time.

The email list converts. The paid traffic converts. The sync deals close or they don’t — but the pitches are already in. The partnerships are active. The catalog is working.

Justin Welsh hit his first $100K quarter in under 80 days using a single $150 digital product to a 14,000-person email list. No team. No ads. No investors. Just a 90-day content and product sprint documented in real time. The model is proven. Execution is the variable.

This is the month where execution vindicates the plan or exposes the gaps. Either way, the documentation continues.

The Five Moves That Get Me There

Every dollar in this plan traces back to one of five moves. Not twenty. Not a hundred. Five.

Move 1 — Catalog Activation

Every title on beacons.ai/gorelentless needs to work 24/7 like a soldier — not sit there like a souvenir.

Twenty-five-plus books is not an inventory problem. It’s an activation problem. The catalog needs SEO, storytelling, and context — for first-time readers discovering it now and for the reader who finished PUSH in 2003 and hasn’t come back since. Both audiences are real. Both are reachable.

Move 2 — Content as Commerce

Every post on relentlessaaron.net is a conversion opportunity.

Not in a pitch-every-sentence way. In the way that story-driven content sells without selling — because the story is the proof. When I write about building from a prison cell, I’m not running a campaign. I’m telling the truth. And the truth converts.

Myron Golden publicly documented his offer ladder methodology in 2022 and turned income transparency into a $2.3 million content cycle. The transparency wasn’t vulnerability. It was strategy. That’s the model running here.

Every article is a chapter in a live business story. Readers who follow along become customers. Customers become community. Community becomes the economy.

Move 3 — Sync Licensing

The music catalog has IP that belongs in TV, film, and digital media.

One placement changes the quarter. So the pitches go out — every one of them — regardless of odds. This is the high-upside play that gets worked in Month 2 and potentially closes in Month 3.

Move 4 — Community Activation

The readers who found PLATINUM DOLLS, TOPLESS, LADY FIRST, and SINGLE WITH BENEFITS through word of mouth — through prison libraries, a cousin at a cookout, a copy passed down a cellblock — are a built-in economy waiting to be activated.

They didn’t need an algorithm to tell them what was good. They already knew.

Activating that community means giving them a reason to come back, a way to engage, and a way to spend money on content they already love. Bundle deals, exclusive drops, community events — those are the mechanisms.

Move 5 — Strategic Partnerships

Black creators, urban fiction platforms, cultural media that already carries the audience I’m building toward.

This isn’t co-signing for clout. It’s building distribution. Every partner who sends their audience to the Beacons store extends the reach of the catalog without extending the ad budget. Strategic alignment is leverage. Leverage closes the gap between $20K and $100K in 90 days.

What Could Go Wrong — And How I’m Planning for It

90 days is tight. Life doesn’t pause for your plan.

Health. Relationships. Unexpected costs. Distractions with teeth. These are real variables that don’t care about your spreadsheet.

The mitigation is simple: weekly check-ins, published on this site. Hard numbers. Honest assessment. No spin. If Month 1 hits $15K instead of $20K, the post says $15K and explains what shifted and what’s adjusting. Hard pivots are allowed. Excuses are not.

The Prison Entrepreneurship Program in Texas reports a 7% recidivism rate against a national 76% average. Researchers point to one variable: the business-building framework. Structure forged under extreme constraint outperforms structure built in comfort — because constraint forces decision clarity that comfort cannot manufacture.

I didn’t go through what I went through to fold under a 90-day business sprint.

If I hit $80K instead of $100K, that’s still movement. But I’m not planning for $80K. There’s no room in my head for $80K. The number is $100,000 in 90 days, and that’s the target every single day.

Coss Marte built ConBody — a prison-style fitness brand — from zero to over $1 million in revenue within 18 months of release. He used the constraint narrative as his core marketing engine. The origin story became the revenue-generating asset. When you’ve operated in genuine scarcity, the story of how you built anyway is the most powerful sales tool you own.

Every article I write about this journey is both documentation and marketing. Both are real. Neither cancels the other out.

What This Has to Do With You

This isn’t just my 90-day plan.

It’s a blueprint being written in real time for anyone with something to sell, a story to tell, or a legacy to build.

The street literature hustle started with hand-to-hand sales from a prison cell — books moving through mail systems, read in rec yards, county holding cells, halfway houses, and apartment buildings that mainstream publishing never thought to reach. If that model worked then, a digital catalog plus a content strategy in 2024 has zero excuse.

76% of Americans live paycheck to paycheck. The $100K milestone isn’t just aspirational — it’s structurally transformative. It changes the household. It changes the options. It changes what you can do for your kids, your community, and your own sense of what’s possible.

BUILD-IN-PUBLIC is not a marketing tactic here. It’s a cultural act of aggression.

When a Black man publicly documents his path to $100K in real time — with real numbers, real losses, real pivots — he’s doing something financial media and legacy gatekeepers have structurally worked to prevent for generations. The Federal Reserve data on Black entrepreneurs being denied small business loans at twice the rate isn’t a footnote. It’s the context for every digital-first move being made here.

The transparency isn’t vulnerability. It’s warfare.

You’ve got your own $100K move sitting in front of you right now. A catalog. A skill. A story. A product. A service. Something working below its potential.

The question is whether you’re willing to say the number out loud.

Because the moment you say it — put it in public, in writing, with a deadline — the relationship with that number changes. It stops being a dream. It becomes a commitment. And commitment, backed by daily action and public accountability, is the closest thing to a guarantee this game offers.

Say the number.
