It’s Possible: How You Can Harness Belief, Resilience, and Determination for Real Success

Les Brown’s motivational framework—beginning with “It’s Possible,” progressing through “It’s Necessary,” “It’s Hard,” “It’s You,” “It’s Worth It,” and culminating in “It’s Done”—has profoundly influenced my journey. Reflecting on how these principles have shaped my life, I recognize their transformative power and believe they can equally empower you to achieve your aspirations. I thought I’d dive deeper into these ideas in hopes that you too will be engaged and transformed.

Les Brown’s powerful message in “It’s Possible” centers on a simple but life-changing idea: belief, perseverance, and self-determination can be the foundation for achieving your goals, no matter what the world throws at you. These principles are at the core of creating a life filled with purpose and progress, even in the face of adversity. Here’s how you can apply these principles in a tangible, teachable way to fuel your own journey forward.

1. Cultivate Self-Belief

The first and most important step in any pursuit is belief in your ability to make it happen. Self-belief is the seed for every action, every achievement, and every breakthrough. Without it, you won’t take the necessary risks, and you won’t commit fully to the work required. To cultivate that belief, start small: tackle something that’s been sitting on your list for too long. Each completed task builds confidence and lays a foundation of trust in yourself.

Tip: Make self-belief a daily practice. Reflect on past successes and how they came to be—remind yourself that you are capable and that your journey has value. Building a personal mantra like “I am capable, and my success is inevitable” can help solidify this belief.

2. Set Clear Goals and Intentions

Big dreams are only dreams until you set them as goals with timelines, actions, and checkpoints. One of the biggest reasons dreams don’t come to life is because they’re too broad or vague. To make them real, break down large aspirations into smaller, achievable objectives that you can address one by one. By having a clear plan of action, you’re creating a roadmap for how to get where you want to go.

Tip: Make your goals SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound). For example, if you want to write a book, start with a specific chapter goal each week. Define your objectives and track your progress relentlessly.

3. Embrace Persistence and Resilience

Persistence means you show up again and again, even on the tough days, even when results aren’t immediate. It’s the fuel that keeps you moving through setbacks and moments of doubt. Embracing resilience isn’t just about bouncing back after challenges but about learning and growing from them.

Remember that every challenge is a chance to build your skills, test your patience, and prepare for the next level. Perseverance isn’t about being perfect; it’s about being relentless in your pursuit of growth, even if growth doesn’t happen overnight.

Tip: Set aside a weekly time to reflect on setbacks as learning experiences. Journaling about what you’ve learned from challenges helps you become more adaptable, building resilience over time.

4. Leverage Creativity for Greater Impact

Sometimes, the pathway to your goal isn’t direct. You’ll need to get creative, think outside of traditional steps, and use your unique skills and experiences to carve a new route. Think about how you can make an impact with your talents beyond just your direct goals. When you connect creativity with purpose, you expand the influence and reach of your work.

Tip: Think about how your work can benefit others. For instance, if you’re an artist, consider using your work to teach or inspire others in your community. Use storytelling to share your journey, lessons, and insights, which can be a powerful way to impact others and give back.

5. Share and Inspire with Your Own Journey

When you work with a mindset grounded in belief and perseverance, others naturally look to you as a guide. Take time to share your experiences, not as a story of “here’s what I did” but rather as an encouragement of “here’s what’s possible for you.” This approach makes your experiences relatable and serves as a blueprint for others to follow.

Tip: Be open to sharing the behind-the-scenes aspects of your journey. Let people see your struggles, breakthroughs, and small wins. Your honesty and transparency make your story accessible and powerful for anyone seeking motivation.

6. Turn Setbacks into Lessons and Fuel

Every setback contains a lesson, and every lesson is an opportunity to come back stronger. The key is to train yourself to see each challenge as a stepping stone. Instead of focusing on the failure, ask yourself, “What can I learn from this? How will this make me better next time?” This mindset ensures that every experience, good or bad, fuels your progress and strengthens your resilience.

Tip: Adopt a growth mindset. When you encounter a problem, approach it with curiosity and an open mind. Problems are puzzles, not roadblocks—challenges designed to refine you and move you closer to your goals.

Moving Forward with “It’s Possible” in Mind

Les Brown’s “It’s Possible” isn’t just a mantra; it’s a way of life. By actively building self-belief, setting clear goals, maintaining resilience, using creativity, and sharing your journey, you turn possibility into progress. Whether you’re creating something entirely new or reaching for a high-stakes goal, the steps are universal: believe, act, learn, share, and repeat.

When you understand that every step forward is a testament to your perseverance, you’ll find the motivation to keep going, even when the path is unclear. No matter where you are in your journey, remember that it’s possible. It’s always possible.
