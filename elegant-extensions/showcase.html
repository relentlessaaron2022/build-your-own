Why it was built this way

People do not only search for “a hair salon in San Diego.” They search for a method: GLAMLOCKS, Great Lengths, tape-ins, Hairdreams, a weave, or a repair. A single homepage cannot answer all of those searches as clearly as a dedicated page can.

That is why each method has its own page. Google and the AI tools people use to ask questions can then match a specific search to a specific, factual page that names Elegant Extensions, states that you work in San Diego and La Jolla, and puts (858) 945-4247 in easy reach.

The La Jolla studio page is the only location page with a street address, because 7520 La Jolla Blvd is the address on your listings and on your LinkedIn profile. Del Mar and Mission Valley are named on your official site, but a dedicated page for either one was held until a current address is confirmed.

Click-to-text and click-to-call are on every page because the point of this work is a consultation, not a long browse. There is no checkout on these pages.

Only facts from your official site, the GlamLocks “Meet Waatani” biography, and your LinkedIn profile were used for claims about the business. Your official wording is followed for certifications (about 15 techniques, about ten certifications), for GLAMLOCKS and GL7 co-creation, and for America’s Next Top Model, KUSI, and North County Times mentions. LinkedIn’s “since April 1995” is attributed to that profile, not invented.

Facts left off, pending your confirmation

These items showed up in research or on older or third-party listings, but not clearly enough to publish as current fact. Nothing below has been added as a hard claim. If you want any of it public, say the word and it goes on. If you want it kept off, it stays off.

  1. Dollar prices. An older third-party service list quoted figures such as $595, $795, and $1,595. Those may be stale. The pricing page explains what a quote includes. It does not publish a price list.
  2. A “free consultation and 25% off” offer. That wording appears on your official homepage. It was not copied here, in case the offer has changed. The booking page tells people they can ask about current consultation offers when they text.
  3. A dedicated Del Mar page, including 1555 Camino del Mar, Suite 203. That address appears on older listings and on the GlamLocks biography, sometimes with a different phone number. It is held until you confirm the chair is current. The footer notes Del Mar appointments and asks people to text to confirm.
  4. A dedicated Mission Valley page. Your official site names the area. No street address was found. The footer notes Mission Valley appointments, with a request to text to confirm.
  5. A second phone number, (858) 800-4078. It appeared on one third-party salon page only. It is omitted.
  6. Braid-style services — knotless braids, box braids, men’s braids, or kids’ braids. Your public brand is hair extensions, not a braiding shop. Those pages were not built.
  7. Google ratings and review counts. Public listings do not agree with each other, and some reviews are old. No star rating or review count is on the pages.
